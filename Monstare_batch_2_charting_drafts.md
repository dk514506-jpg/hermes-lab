## A9-01 — Mark, Gudith & Klocke (2008) The Cost of Interrupted Work
- Key Finding / Thesis: Empirical experiment on interruption context: context of interruptions does not make a difference, but people completed interrupted tasks in LESS time with no difference in quality; people compensate for interruptions by working faster, at the price of more stress, higher frustration, time pressure, and effort; individual differences matter (openness to experience and need for personal structure predict disruption costs).
- Effect Size / Strength: not_quantifiable from abstract (directional; specific statistics in full text).
- Limitations: Lab setting with artificial tasks; student/university participants; self-reported stress/effort; single-session; compensation-via-speed may mask long-term costs.
- Disconfirming Implication: if quality metrics were insensitive to compensation effects, the "no quality difference" finding underestimates cost; speed-accuracy tradeoffs mean faster completion is not free; individual differences (personality moderators) limit average-effect generalization.
- H1: yes | H2: yes
- Design Implication: Workday Interruption Protocol: interruptions carry hidden costs (stress, effort, time pressure) even when output is preserved; support resumption and reduce interruption-driven compensation (e.g., batch/queue interruptions, resumption cues).
- Cosmotechnic Implication: Interruption architecture is a technics of attention: the tool's interruption regime shapes the person's attentional cosmos (what may interrupt, when, and at what felt cost); Tier-P.
- Causal Status: causal (experiment)

## A9-02 — Gonzalez & Mark (2004) Multi-tasking Craziness / Working Spheres
- Key Finding / Thesis: Fieldwork observation of information workers (analysts, developers, managers): people organize work in larger, thematically connected units ("working spheres"), not single tasks; high discontinuity of activity: ~3 minutes per task, ~2+ minutes per tool before switching; ~10 working spheres per person, ~12 minutes per sphere before switching; IT should support continual switching between working spheres.
- Effect Size / Strength: not_quantifiable (descriptive observational metrics: minutes-per-task, minutes-per-sphere).
- Limitations: Observational fieldwork with small role-specific samples; descriptive, not inferential; context-specific (2004 knowledge-work tooling; three roles); working spheres is an interpretive construct, not directly measured.
- Disconfirming Implication: switching rates may be artifacts of the 2004 tool environment (not generalizable to modern tooling); if working spheres is a researcher interpretation, users may not organize work that way; discontinuity could reflect organizational context rather than inherent work structure.
- H1: yes | H2: partial (attention-ecology dimension; not full vector)
- Design Implication: Workday Interruption Protocol: design for working spheres (thematic units of work) rather than isolated tasks; support sphere-switching, sphere resumption, and cross-sphere continuity; avoid task-centric single-window assumptions.
- Cosmotechnic Implication: Working spheres are the person's own organizational cosmos; the tool must respect and mirror the user's sphere structure rather than imposing task-centric design; locality of the user's own order (S10-adjacent).
- Causal Status: correlational (observational)

## A9-04 — Leroy (2009) Attention Residue
- Key Finding / Thesis: Two experiments: people need to stop thinking about one task to fully transition attention and perform well on another; it is difficult to disengage attention from an unfinished task, and subsequent task performance suffers; finishing the task before switching is NOT enough — time pressure while finishing is needed to disengage from the first task, and it contributes to higher performance on the next task.
- Effect Size / Strength: not_quantifiable from abstract (lab performance measures; specific statistics in full text, which is paywalled).
- Limitations: Abstract-level charting (full text institutional via ScienceDirect); lab experiments; artificial tasks; the time-pressure finding is counterintuitive and may be boundary-limited (task type, pressure magnitude).
- Disconfirming Implication: Time pressure as a disengagement aid sits in tension with A9-01's stress/effort costs of pressure — the two effects may trade off; if residue depends on interruption/task type, effects may not generalize; "finish before switching" advice is explicitly insufficient, challenging simple task-closure recommendations.
- H1: yes | H2: partial (attention-residue dimension of the quality vector)
- Design Implication: Arousal/Flow/Task-Switching Layer: support task closure and disengagement rituals; attention residue is a cost of switching that persists after task completion; design resumption aids and closure affordances; treat time-pressure levers with stress caveats (cf. A9-01).
- Cosmotechnic Implication: Attention residue is a phenomenological remainder — the unfinished task haunts the next; the tool's switch-design shapes the continuity of the person's attention and the felt weight of the unfinished (Tier-P).
- Causal Status: causal (experiments)

## A9-05 — Arnold, Goldschmitt & Rigotti (2023) Information Overload review
- Key Finding / Thesis: PRISMA systematic review of prevention/intervention measures for information overload: 87 studies/field reports/conceptual papers included; many interventions at the behavioral prevention level; structural prevention proposals also numerous (work design at ICT level and at teamwork/organizational-regulation level); the strength of evidence across studies is MIXED.
- Effect Size / Strength: not_quantifiable (systematic review; no pooled ES; mixed evidence strength is the headline finding).
- Limitations: Mixed/heterogeneous evidence base; many included items are conceptual papers or field reports, not controlled studies; behavioral-level interventions numerous but weak evidence; structural-level proposals often untested; review scope limited to prevention/intervention literature.
- Disconfirming Implication: If evidence strength is mixed, no single intervention is validated — pilot must not import any specific intervention as proven; if most structural proposals are untested, work-design guidance is provisional; digitalization framing may embed normative assumptions about information environments.
- H1: partial | H2: yes (ecology dimension: information environment as part of the attention cosmos)
- Design Implication: Workday Interruption Protocol: multi-level intervention menu (behavioral + structural: ICT design, teamwork/organizational regulation); expect mixed evidence; pilot should measure its own outcomes rather than assuming intervention efficacy.
- Cosmotechnic Implication: Information overload is a milieu condition: the tool must reduce informational noise as part of cultivating the person's attentional cosmos — structural (not just behavioral) responsibility; Tier-P.
- Causal Status: conceptual (systematic review)

## A9-06 — Rick, Brandl, Mertens & Nitsch (2024) Work Interruptions of Office Workers
- Key Finding / Thesis: 492 German office workers, one-day diary study: interruption frequency -> subjective workload is significantly MEDIATED by the individual's perception of the interruptions; the complexity of the primary work task MODERATES the effect — more complex primary tasks strengthen the positive relationship between interruption frequency and subjective workload.
- Effect Size / Strength: not_quantifiable from abstract (significant mediation and moderation; specific coefficients in full text, which is paywalled — IOS/SAGE).
- Limitations: Diary self-report (subjective workload and interruption perception); single-day diary; cross-sectional mediation limits causal inference; German office-worker sample; complexity operationalization not detailed in abstract.
- Disconfirming Implication: Perception mediation implies interruption APPRAISAL is the operative locus — interventions could target appraisal rather than (only) reducing interruptions; complexity moderation means low-complexity phases are less affected, so blanket interruption bans may be overkill; diary design cannot establish causal order.
- H1: yes | H2: partial (subjective perception dimension)
- Design Implication: Workday Interruption Protocol: protect complex-task phases from interruption (complexity moderator); treat interruption perception/appraisal as an intervention target; subjective workload as a key outcome metric; context-sensitive interruption policy rather than blanket rules.
- Cosmotechnic Implication: The perceived interruption is the locus of cost: the phenomenology of interruption (appraisal) shapes workload as much as the interruption itself — the tool's interruption grammar is co-constitutive of the person's experienced work cosmos; Tier-P.
- Causal Status: correlational (diary mediation; cross-sectional)

## CORE-09 — Carver & Scheier (1982) Control Theory / Self-Regulation
- Key Finding / Thesis: Self-regulation operates through cybernetic feedback control: behavior is regulated by comparing current state against reference standards; discrepancies motivate corrective action (negative feedback loops); control operates across a hierarchy of levels (from motor programs to behavioral systems to self); the framework unifies personality, social, clinical, and health psychology; self-awareness engages the self-referential level, intensifying discrepancy monitoring; where discrepancy reduction is impeded, withdrawal/rumination may follow.
- Effect Size / Strength: not_quantifiable (theoretical framework article; no pooled ES).
- Limitations: Conceptual synthesis; cybernetic metaphor carries information-processing assumptions; predictions require specifying reference values and loop parameters; the framework is not empirically tested as a whole in this article; later work adds approach/positive-feedback dynamics beyond pure discrepancy reduction.
- Disconfirming Implication: If habitual/automatic processes dominate regulation, the model overstates conscious feedback control; if discrepancy reduction is not the only regulation mode (approach systems, e.g., Carver & Scheier's later additions), the negative-feedback framing is incomplete; self-focus can produce withdrawal rather than correction when reduction is blocked.
- H1: yes | H2: partial
- Design Implication: Arousal/Flow/Task-Switching Layer: standards-based self-monitoring as the grammar of self-regulation; support reference-setting (goal standards), discrepancy feedback, and resumption of corrective action; design for the hierarchy (task-level loops within self-level standards).
- Cosmotechnic Implication: Self-regulation as governed return: the feedback loop is the person's own grammar of return to their affirmed order — the tool should support the person's standards, not substitute its own; medium Cosmo Rel.
- Causal Status: conceptual (theoretical framework)

## CORE-10 — Locke & Latham (2002) Goal-Setting Theory
- Key Finding / Thesis: 35 years of research: specific and difficult goals lead to higher performance than easy or vague ("do your best") goals, given goal commitment, feedback, and task-appropriate strategy; goals operate through four mechanisms (direction of attention, effort, persistence, strategy development); moderators include goal commitment, feedback, and task complexity; goals mediate the effects of incentives; external validity and practical significance are emphasized; limitations of the theory are discussed.
- Effect Size / Strength: not_quantifiable from charted source (review article; the underlying program's meta-analytic support is cited within the field but not pooled in this article; per batch convention, do not import external ES).
- Limitations: Review article (not a new study); goal effects strongest for simple tasks — for complex tasks, learning goals ("learn how") outperform performance goals; assumes conscious, rational goal pursuit; potential negative effects of goals (tunnel vision, unethical shortcuts — cf. CORE-11 "Goals Gone Wild") are not the focus here.
- Disconfirming Implication: If goals narrow attention excessively in complex or creative work, performance and quality suffer (goal-performance relation inverts); goal effects are conditional on commitment and feedback — without them, difficult goals can backfire; incentives interact with goals (cf. CORE-05/CORE-06 crowding: externally imposed goals may crowd internal commitment).
- H1: yes | H2: partial
- Design Implication: Monstare Protocol vNext: goal specificity and difficulty as levers, gated on commitment, feedback, and task complexity; use learning goals for complex tasks; watch the interaction with incentives (crowding risk per CORE-05/06); avoid goal imposition (telos stays the person's own).
- Cosmotechnic Implication: Goal-setting is a technics of directed attention: specific-difficult goals shape the direction and persistence of attention; the goal's telos must remain the person's own (S1 guard) — externally imposed goals risk becoming output instruments; medium Cosmo Rel.
- Causal Status: causal (review of an experimental program; goal-difficulty effects experimentally established)

## CORE-15 — Gross (1998) Emotion Regulation Timeline
- Key Finding / Thesis: Process model distinguishes antecedent-focused (reappraisal) from response-focused (suppression) emotion regulation; experiment: 120 participants watched a disgusting film; both reappraisal and suppression reduced emotion-expressive behavior; reappraisal decreased the EXPERIENCE of disgust; suppression INCREASED sympathetic activation (physiological cost); the two processes have different adaptive consequences.
- Effect Size / Strength: not_quantifiable from abstract (significant behavioral/experiential/physiological differences; specific statistics in full text, which is institutional).
- Limitations: Abstract-level charting (full text institutional via APA); lab emotion induction (film); single emotion (disgust); short-term measurement; suppression's costs may accumulate over time (not tested here); reappraisal may require cognitive resources that fail under load.
- Disconfirming Implication: Suppression's increased sympathetic activation implies health/attentional costs of response-focused regulation; if reappraisal requires resources, it may be unavailable under cognitive load (relevant to interruption-heavy work); strategy effectiveness may depend on context and individual differences not covered here.
- H1: yes | H2: partial
- Design Implication: Monstare Protocol vNext: emotion regulation is time-sensitive — support ANTECEDENT-focused regulation (reappraisal, situation modification) over response-focused (suppression); regulation timing (before vs after the emotion) is a design parameter; avoid designs that force suppression (e.g., no outlet for frustration in interruption flows).
- Cosmotechnic Implication: Emotion regulation grammar: antecedent-focused regulation respects the person's experience (works with the felt cosmos), response-focused suppression works against it at a physiological cost; the tool's temporal structure (when it interrupts, when it asks for emotion work) is morally and experientially consequential; medium Cosmo Rel.
- Causal Status: causal (experiment)
