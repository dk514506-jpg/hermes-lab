# Premature Coherence Audit (Batch 4-5)

After charting 70 rows across batches 4-5, the user flagged that premature coherence had crept into the work. This reference documents the specific instances identified and the corrective measures to prevent recurrence.

## What is premature coherence?

Premature coherence is the tendency to make ideas fit together before they've been given a chance to clash. In evidence-matrix work, it shows up as:
- **Flattening**: reducing an argument to the same cells as every other row
- **Smoothing**: naming tensions but resolving them into "complementarity" rather than letting them stand as genuine conflicts
- **Extracting for use**: reading authors for their design implications rather than engaging with their full argument
- **Imposing through-lines**: discovering cross-cutting themes that make the matrix sound like a unified body when it may be a site of irresolvable disagreement

## Specific instances identified (batches 4-5)

### 1. The "organism" framing is false
The batch 4ab synthesis presented 18 A1 authors as "a single argument in multiple specializations" with "organs" (cosmos-morality unification, genesis of technical objects, pharmacological warning...). This is elegant but misleading. These authors are not organs of one body — they're combatants on a battlefield. Simondon vs. Winner, Stiegler vs. Heidegger, Hui vs. Haraway are not complementary specializations but potentially *incommensurable* positions.

### 2. The "through-lines" are impositions
I claimed the A1 spine has three through-lines (pharmacological/dose, genesis-process, politics-ethics). These aren't discoveries — they're reading frameworks I brought to the texts. Not every author is "really" talking about dose or genesis.

### 3. Every tension gets resolved into complementarity
The fault lines section of every memo named a tension, then resolved it: "apply both," "hold the tension," "the truth is between them." This is premature coherence. Some tensions should stand as *irreconcilable*.

### 4. Design Implications are almost always constructive
Nearly every row says "the tool must..." or "the tool should..." This is extracting for use — reading every author as a design resource. I'm not capturing what authors *refuse*, *negate*, or *leave unresolved*.

### 5. The Limitations cell confesses source weakness
I consistently note "single-author work," "programmatic agenda," "archive mirror" — but rarely capture internal contradictions, unsupported claims, or questionable premises.

### 6. The Epithet Register is underused
I seeded it with 3 examples but haven't populated it for the other 67+ charted rows. I'm capturing theses but not voices.

### 7. The weight flattening
Every batch memo says "zero rows load-bearing on evidence weight." This obscures real differences — Norton & Gino's causal evidence for ritual is not the same weight as Van Gennep's anthropological theory.

## Corrective measures (batch 6+)

1. **Add an "Incommensurability" flag** to Discovery Notes for rows that genuinely cannot be reconciled with other rows
2. **Populate the Epithet Register** for each batch (top 3-5 voices)
3. **Let at least one tension per batch stand unresolved**
4. **Occasionally use negative design implications** ("the tool must NOT...")
5. **Add "Argumentative Limitations"** as a secondary limitations field where warranted
6. **Revisit the batch 4ab synthesis** and revise the organism framing to acknowledge incommensurability
7. **Run a "premature coherence audit"** after each batch — review synthesis memos and fault lines for smoothing

## Example: Incommensurability flag format

```
INCOMMENSURABLE with A1-11 (Winner): Simondon's technical-object genesis (internal normativity) vs. Winner's artifact-politics (maker's politics). If the tool's internal logic is genuinely normative, Winner's inscription is not the whole story. If the maker's politics is the whole story, Simondon's internal normativity is overstated. Genuine philosophical disagreement about the nature of technics — NOT complementary.
```

## Key lesson

The matrix format coerces every argument into the same cells. This is a feature (comparability) and a bug (flattening). Future memos should note: "The matrix format coerces every argument into the same cells; authors whose arguments resist this structure include [X, Y, Z]."
