# Financial Economics and Market Microstructure Research

## When to use

Use when researching arbitrage, statistical arbitrage, market microstructure, price discovery, liquidity, transaction costs, latency, market impact, or execution. Produce a primary-source literature map and translate each result into data, modeling, and validation requirements for an arbitrage system.

## Workflow

1. **Decompose the question by mechanism.** Separate limits to arbitrage; adverse selection and spreads; price discovery; liquidity/illiquidity; latency and market design; transaction costs; dynamic market impact; optimal execution; and statistical-arbitrage signal construction.
2. **Prioritize primary and authoritative records.** Prefer journal publisher pages, learned-society pages, university author pages, NBER, RePEc/EconPapers, and open-access repositories. Verify title, authors, journal, year, pages, and DOI or persistent identifier. A search snippet is discovery evidence, not sufficient support for a substantive claim.
3. **Retrieve and register sources before drafting.** Use the grounded-citations ledger. Register each URL at retrieval time; do not reconstruct URLs from memory. If multiple URLs describe the same paper, choose one authoritative landing page for the main citation and keep duplicate metadata records separate only when useful.
4. **Write cite-while-drafting.** Put citations immediately after supported claims. Include short prose synthesis in addition to tables because citation-coverage tools may ignore table rows. Keep at most three source IDs per sentence; split dense claims into separate sentences.
5. **Translate theory into database requirements.** For every paper, state which fields, timestamps, event joins, cost components, assumptions, and stress tests are needed to avoid overstating tradability.
6. **Separate return layers.** Report theoretical/gross signal return, executable pre-cost return, and realized net P&L. Apply costs to both legs and model asynchronous fills, partial fills, borrow, financing, fees, and impact.
7. **Verify.** Run strict citation verification, inspect warnings about unused ledger entries or over-cited sentences, and fix them before delivery. A table-heavy report can still fail a minimum-coverage threshold; add cited prose rather than weakening the evidence standard.

## Core translation rules

- Midpoint signals are not executable prices: retain bid, ask, quote age, venue, trade condition, and exchange/receipt timestamps.
- Adverse selection requires signed trades and post-trade markouts at several horizons.
- Impact requires size relative to displayed depth/ADV, order-book changes, participation, and recovery/resiliency observations.
- Latency requires exchange time, local receipt time, sequence number, signal time, order submit/ack/cancel/fill times, and clock-normalization metadata.
- Statistical arbitrage requires point-in-time universes, formation/trading windows, as-of joins, hedge-ratio versions, delisting-inclusive data, corporate-action handling, and out-of-sample evaluation.
- Limits-to-arbitrage requires capital, margin, borrow, financing, drawdown, forced-exit, and convergence-horizon fields.

## Deliverable structure

1. Executive synthesis.
2. Mechanism-by-mechanism table: paper, finding, limitation, database implication.
3. Minimum data model and execution metrics.
4. Research controls and failure modes.
5. DOI/persistent-URL source list generated by the citation ledger.

## Pitfalls

- Do not cite a search-result description as though the full paper was inspected.
- Do not treat a low-frequency liquidity proxy as a tick-level execution model.
- Do not assume simultaneous execution of arbitrage legs.
- Do not hide survivorship, look-ahead, stale-quote, or borrow assumptions inside a single headline Sharpe ratio.
- Do not hand-edit source numbering or the generated Sources block.

## FactSet-style product framing

When the user is designing a domain-specific terminal rather than a general trading engine, first translate the target product into analytical workflows: quote board, opportunity screener, portfolio/exposure, attribution, benchmark-relative views, risk/data-quality controls, and scenario/stress analysis. Keep the research synthesis tied to those screens and to a normalized security/instrument master. Separate a theoretical opportunity from an executable opportunity and from realized P&L; never let a generic “price” field stand in for a quote, a buylist, or a transaction.

## Cross-domain architecture

For intersections with cybernetics, information systems, and database management, use a closed-loop framing: sources are sensors, normalized market state is the estimator, strategy/routing is the controller, and fills, slippage, stale-data alerts, rejects, and P&L are feedback. Treat provenance, observability, and data quality as decision controls, not only ETL concerns. Use immutable raw events plus normalized and derived projections; preserve event, receive, ingest, available, valid, and known times. See `references/factset-cybernetics-database.md` for the session-tested translation and source notes.
