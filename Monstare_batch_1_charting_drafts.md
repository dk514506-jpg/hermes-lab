## CORE-01 — Ryan & Deci (2000) SDT
- Key Finding / Thesis: Three innate psychological needs (competence, autonomy, relatedness) are the nutriments of intrinsic motivation and internalization; OIT continuum from external regulation to integrated regulation; CET specifies conditions that facilitate/undermine intrinsic motivation; autonomy is explicitly NOT independence/individualism; needs theorized as universal but socioculturally shaped.
- Effect Size / Strength: Qualitative/theoretical (framework article). Strength = high as foundational construct; quantitative anchor lives in CORE-05, not here.
- Limitations: 2000 overview article (2017 Guilford book more complete); three-need postulate is organismic-theoretical, not independently proven in-article; universality asserted with cross-cultural support cited but not demonstrated here; need satisfaction not operationalized; causal language theoretical.
- Disconfirming Implication: if needs are not universal/distinct, the need-support profile (M2) loses specificity; if autonomy-support effects run through competence/relatedness, autonomy may be over-weighted; CET applies only to activities with initial intrinsic interest; OIT means controlled regulation is not categorically pathological — "extrinsic = bad" is the wrong binary (relative autonomy is the operative distinction).
- H1: yes | H2: yes (quality/ecology vector reinforced; volition-within-context aligns with Hui pluralism)
- Design Implication: ground the need-support profile (M2): competence (optimal challenge, effectance-promoting feedback), autonomy (choice, volition, meaningful rationale, freedom from demeaning evaluation), relatedness (secure connection) as three distinguishable supports.
- Cosmotechnic Implication: motivation quality is the substrate of moral authorship (S1 telic); autonomy-as-volition-within-context prevents Western-individualist import; consistent with Hui cosmotechnic pluralism and A10 cross-cultural rows.
- Causal Status: conceptual (framework article; causal claims are postulates within the theory, supported by the broader program).

## CORE-02 — Markland, Ryan, Tobin & Rollnick (2005) MI + SDT
- Key Finding / Thesis: MI and SDT share the assumption of an innate human tendency toward growth and psychological integration; MI provides the social-environmental facilitating factors SDT specifies (autonomy support, competence, relatedness) for promoting this tendency; SDT offers a coherent theoretical framework for understanding MI's processes and efficacy; MI is an autonomy-supportive clinical method whose effects can be understood through internalization (movement from external to self-determined regulation).
- Effect Size / Strength: Qualitative/theoretical (framework proposal). No quantitative ES in this paper.
- Limitations: Theoretical synthesis, not an empirical test; parallels are drawn, causal pathways proposed but not demonstrated here; MI's empirical efficacy is cited from elsewhere; the authors note MI "lacks a coherent theoretical framework" and offer one — the fit remains to be tested; no controlled data in this article.
- Disconfirming Implication: if MI's efficacy does not operate through need-support/internalization pathways, the SDT framing misdescribes the mechanism; if brief interventions change behavior without autonomy support, the social-environmental account is incomplete; MI outcomes may partly reflect non-SDT mechanisms (therapeutic alliance, expectancy, goal priming).
- H1: yes | H2: partial (autonomy/internalization quality vector; does not itself engage ecology/technology/reversibility dimensions)
- Design Implication: autonomy-preserving conversational method for ambivalence (Self-Nudging Design Card): MI-style reflective listening, empathy, rolling with resistance, evoking the person's own reasons — non-coercive change method; supports internalization rather than compliance.
- Cosmotechnic Implication: non-coercive change as cosmotechnic constraint — a "technics of listening" whose grammar preserves the other's autonomy; medium Cosmo Rel.
- Causal Status: conceptual (theoretical framework proposal).

## CORE-04 — Bandura (1997) Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control [charted via 1994 companion chapter]
- Key Finding / Thesis: Self-efficacy beliefs (domain-specific judgments of capability) are the most central mechanism of human agency ("none is more central or pervasive"); four principal sources — enactive mastery experience (strongest), vicarious experience, verbal/social persuasion, physiological/affective states; efficacy beliefs influence choice, effort, persistence, thought patterns, and emotional states; reciprocal determinism between efficacy and performance; efficacy is domain-specific, not a global trait.
- Effect Size / Strength: Theoretical synthesis (book-level). Qualitative; the book marshals meta-analytic support for efficacy-performance relations (e.g., Stajkovic & Luthans 1998) but no single ES is claimed by the source itself. Strength = high as a construct anchor; note domain-specificity.
- Limitations: Self-report measurement is the dominant operationalization; causal direction is reciprocal (efficacy ↔ performance), not unidirectional; domain-specificity limits simple global assessment; misjudged efficacy (overconfidence) can be harmful (poor preparation, risky action); the 1997 book predates later measurement and replication debates; the row's readable copy is archive-lending — charting used Bandura's 1994 companion chapter (official site) as the accessible statement of the same theory.
- Disconfirming Implication: if efficacy beliefs are largely epiphenomenal reflections of prior performance, the causal claim weakens; efficacy-performance correlations may reflect common method variance (self-report × self-report) or ability confounds; interventions that raise efficacy without performance gains suggest boundary conditions; overconfidence effects cut against "more efficacy is better".
- H1: yes | H2: partial (competence dimension vector; not the full H2 vector)
- Design Implication: build efficacy via early mastery, not exhortation (M2): graded success experiences, modeling, credible persuasive feedback, physiological-state interpretation; avoid pure encouragement without mastery.
- Cosmotechnic Implication: competence as capacity for good action — efficacy beliefs are the felt capacity to act well within a cosmos; medium Cosmo Rel.
- Causal Status: causal as asserted within social-cognitive theory; the accessible source base is largely self-report/correlational plus lab experiments — Methodologist to adjudicate.

## CORE-05 — Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999) meta-analysis
- Key Finding / Thesis: Meta-analysis of 128 experiments: engagement-contingent (d = −0.40), completion-contingent (d = −0.36), and performance-contingent (d = −0.28) rewards significantly undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation; all rewards, all tangible rewards, and all expected rewards also undermined free-choice; engagement- and completion-contingent rewards undermined self-reported interest (d = −0.15, −0.17); positive feedback enhanced free-choice behavior (d = 0.33) and self-reported interest (d = 0.31); tangible rewards more detrimental for children than college students; verbal rewards less enhancing for children.
- Effect Size / Strength: Small-to-medium negative effects (d ≈ −0.28 to −0.40 free-choice; −0.15/−0.17 self-report); positive feedback small positive (d ≈ 0.31–0.33). This is the batch's quantitative anchor.
- Limitations: Meta-analytic heterogeneity; laboratory-task heavy; free-choice vs self-report measures capture different outcomes; task-type and reward-type moderators; mostly Western/educational samples; single-subject studies without no-reward controls excluded; the paper explicitly reviews 4 previous meta-analyses with divergent results (e.g., Cameron & Pierce 1994 found weaker/no undermining), so effect estimates are contested.
- Disconfirming Implication: undermining applies to activities with initial intrinsic interest (CET boundary); unexpected vs expected rewards differ (expectation matters); if field settings show weaker crowding than lab free-choice measures, external validity is limited; divergent prior meta-analyses mean the true effect size is debated; rewards for uninteresting tasks do not undermine and can enhance.
- H1: yes | H2: yes (quality/ecology vector: reward structure shapes motivational quality, not just output)
- Design Implication: sets c_C (crowding coefficient) in the model — Motivation Crowding Watch Card: avoid contingent tangible rewards for intrinsically motivated activity; prefer autonomy-supportive feedback (which enhances); watch reward expectation.
- Cosmotechnic Implication: buying compliance can cost autonomy = cosmotechnic failure; incentive design is morally consequential (reward structures are technics that reshape the person's relation to the activity).
- Causal Status: causal (meta-analysis of experiments).

## CORE-06 — Frey & Jegen (2001) motivation crowding theory
- Key Finding / Thesis: External interventions via monetary incentives or punishments may undermine ("crowd out") intrinsic motivation, and under identifiable conditions strengthen ("crowd in") it; crowding-out is contingent on whether the intervention is perceived as controlling vs acknowledging; the paper demonstrates empirical relevance through circumstantial evidence, laboratory studies, and field cases (e.g., blood donation, Gneezy & Rustichini fine experiments, psychological experiments).
- Effect Size / Strength: Qualitative review/synthesis of empirical evidence; no single pooled ES; cites specific studies' directional effects (crowding-out and crowding-in).
- Limitations: Narrative review, not meta-analysis; "circumstantial evidence" acknowledged by the authors; heterogeneous study designs; identification of crowding effects in field settings is difficult; the senior author is the theory's proponent (advocacy consideration); some flagship cases (blood donation) are contested in later literature; crowding-in conditions are less studied.
- Disconfirming Implication: if extrinsic incentives do not reliably undermine intrinsic motivation outside the lab (cf. CORE-05/Cameron-Pierce divergence), crowding-out may be context-limited; standard economic predictions (incentives work) hold in many markets — crowding needs identifiable conditions; if perceived-control is the operative variable, the phenomenon may be a mediation effect rather than a distinct "crowding" process.
- H1: yes | H2: yes (ecology vector: incentive structure is part of the motivational environment)
- Design Implication: cross-disciplinary confirmation of crowding risk — Motivation Crowding Watch Card; treat incentive addition as a morally and motivationally consequential act, monitor for crowding-out signals.
- Cosmotechnic Implication: incentive design is morally consequential — economic technics shape the motivational cosmos; a tool that pays for what was freely done re-orders the person's relation to the act.
- Causal Status: correlational (narrative review; the lab experiments cited are causal, the synthesis is not).

## CORE-08 — Reijula & Hertwig (2020/2022) self-nudging
- Key Finding / Thesis: Nudges can often be turned into self-nudges: empowering interventions that enable people to design and structure their own decision environments — to act as citizen choice architects; self-nudging applies behavioral insights without paternalism/manipulation concerns; expands behavioral insights from the public to the personal sphere (homes, offices, families); tools include designing one's proximate choice architecture (defaults, repositioning, notifications, friction), education about the nature/causes of self-control problems, and self-paternalistic interventions (hard constraints on oneself that do not curb others' liberty); online environments' manipulative properties can be harnessed for self-control (defaults, greyscale, ad blockers, removing devices).
- Effect Size / Strength: Qualitative/theoretical with illustrative case studies (organic food-box scheme, browser defaults, marshmallow-test tactics); no pooled ES; evidence base emerging.
- Limitations: Much evidence is illustrative rather than experimental; self-nudging presupposes self-knowledge and some education about choice architecture; not sufficient for all self-control failures (deep addiction, compulsion); boundary between self-nudge and self-paternalism is ethically underexplored; effectiveness depends on environment malleability and sustained user engagement.
- Disconfirming Implication: if people systematically fail to anticipate their own future behavior (meta-level present bias), self-nudges will be under-deployed or mis-designed; if environments are not malleable, self-nudging is unavailable; self-nudging could entrench an individual-responsibility narrative that shifts blame from harmful environments to individuals (moral-hollowing risk — Ethics Auditor to check); some domains (strong habits) resist choice-architecture fixes.
- H1: yes | H2: yes (authorship/quality vector; self-authorship as vehicle for autonomy — but seed: not sufficient)
- Design Implication: central mechanism for authorship (lambda; M4d) — Self-Nudging Design Card: enable the user to redesign their own choice architecture (defaults, notification regimes, friction, positioning) rather than only being nudged.
- Cosmotechnic Implication: self-authorship as vehicle for autonomy, but not sufficient — the tool must let the user be choice-architect of their own environment, consistent with locality-not-localism (S10).
- Causal Status: conceptual (theoretical/design proposal).

## A1-01 — Hui (2016) The Question Concerning Technology in China
- Key Finding / Thesis: Technology is not anthropologically universal; "cosmotechnics" names the unification of cosmos (cosmological order), morality (ethical order), and technics (technical order) — "scientific and technical thinking emerges under cosmological conditions expressed in the relations between humans and their milieus, which are never static" (p.37 of archive copy); the question concerning technology in China cannot be answered by importing Heidegger's Occidental framework (impasse); urgent need for a Chinese philosophy of technology that pluralizes technics and counters blind modernization (超英趕美) while avoiding culturalist nostalgia; critique of Leroi-Gourhan (technical genealogy misses cosmological conditions) and Gille (Western-centric technical systems, ignores Needham's observation of China's historical technical lead).
- Effect Size / Strength: Philosophical/theoretical; no empirical ES. Strength = high as the governing cosmotechnic criterion source.
- Limitations: Single-author constructive essay/book; programmatic argument; selective engagement with Chinese intellectual history (read through Heidegger/Needham/Simondon/Stiegler); the archive.org copy is a scan with rights/edition caution; translation of Chinese sources embedded in argument.
- Disconfirming Implication: if technics are ultimately universalizable (functional convergence), cosmotechnic pluralism overstates divergence; if "cosmotechnics" itself operates with Western categories, the critique of dependency is incomplete (an internal challenge Hui anticipates); historical technology shows massive cross-cultural borrowing, complicating civilizational distinctness claims.
- H1: partial | H2: yes (plurality/ecology/technology vector at the foundation)
- Design Implication: governing telos (layer 2.13) — the plan's horizon: the tool must be grounded in a cosmos-ethic-technics unity, not output maximization; any artifact claim must be checkable against the cosmotechnic criterion.
- Cosmotechnic Implication: sets the whole cosmotechnic criterion; anti-veneer standard — "cosmotechnics" must remain structural, never decorative.
- Causal Status: conceptual (philosophical).

## HUI-2024 — Hui (2024) Machine and Sovereignty
- Key Finding / Thesis: Third and final volume of the Recursivity & Contingency series (after Art and Cosmotechnics 2021); a "Tractatus Politico-Technologicus": political philosophy can no longer avoid technology; introduces "political epistemology" as the method (following Mumford's megamachine — political epistemology legitimates and guides the megamachine); engages Hegel's nation-state and Schmitt's Großraum as the two modern political forms a future planetary thinking must surpass; core agenda: technodiversity (biodiversity–noodiversity–technodiversity triad), an organology of wars, the "Nomos of the Digital Earth", digital sovereignty, the prosthetic future critique (acceleration/automation — prosthesis vs replacement, §32), epistemological diplomacy, locality without localism.
- Effect Size / Strength: Philosophical; no empirical ES. Strength = high as the v6 scaffold/anti-veneer standard.
- Limitations: Programmatic and speculative; dense Hegel/Schmitt exegesis; single-author; the open-access UMN 2024 edition is the verified text (also local copy /opt/data/machine and sovereignty yuk hui.pdf, 352p); claims are architectonic rather than empirically demonstrated.
- Disconfirming Implication: if nation-state and Großraum are not the binding constraints on contemporary digital power (platform/network sovereignty differs from territorial), the planetary framing may miss emergent forms; if technodiversity is read as valorizing any difference (relativism), the anti-localism guard weakens; acceleration/automation may outpace organological critique; if planetary thinking remains a European project in disguise, its universality claim is strained.
- H1: partial | H2: yes (technology/ecology/reversibility vector; technodiversity, organology, prosthesis-vs-replacement)
- Design Implication: govern cosmotechnic criteria: technodiversity, organology/proportion, prosthesis vs replacement, locality/localism — Cosmotechnic Audit Card / Design Memo v6.
- Cosmotechnic Implication: primary v6 cosmotechnic scaffold and anti-veneer standard; the tool must remain a locality capable of revision, not a localism hardened into identity (S10).
- Causal Status: conceptual (philosophical).
