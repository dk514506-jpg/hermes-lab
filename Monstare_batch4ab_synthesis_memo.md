## 1. The stable figure: what emerged

The A1 spine, read as a whole, is a **single argument in multiple specializations**. The underlying problem is: *"How does technics relate to the human, and what does that demand of design?"* Each author reaches a different precision on that question, and together they form an organism — not a list of theses but a living tissue of interconnected positions.

**The organism's anatomy:**

| Organ | Authors | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Cosmos-morality-technics unification** | A1-01 Hui, A1-02 Hui, A1-03 Hui | Establishes the cosmotechnic criterion: technics is the unification of cosmos and morality, and this unification is always *plural* (multiple cosmotechnics, not one universal technology). The Daoist/Tragist typology is a new specialization within this organ. |
| **Genesis of technical objects** | A1-05 Simondon (MESO), A1-06 Simondon (Individuation) | Establishes that technical objects are not inert but evolving relational beings with an internal genesis (concretization). The person is an individuating system, not a fixed agent. These two rows are Simondon's complementary theses — one on technical objects, one on individuation in general. |
| **Pharmacological WARNING** | A1-07 Stiegler | Establishes that technics is the exteriorization of memory through prosthesis, and this exteriorization is *pharmakon* (remedy + poison) from the start. Co-constitution of human and technics. Feeds AI Assistant Use Rules. |
| **Democratic rationalization** | A1-08 Feenberg | Establishes that the design space is politically open — technical rationality contains an indeterminist margin where democratic participation is possible. The warrant for user authorship beyond interface choice. |
| **Perceptual-action mediation** | A1-09 Ihde | Establishes four human-technology relation types (embodiment, hermeneutic, alterity, background) as a design grammar for assessing what the tool does to perception and action. |
| **Moral mediation** | A1-10 Verbeek | Establishes that technologies are *moral mediators* — they shape the moral landscape within which decisions are made. "Script" and "experimental ethics" are the key mechanisms. |
| **Inscribed politics** | A1-11 Winner | Establishes that artifacts have politics — they settle social issues by configuration or require a particular political organization. Feeds Design Veto Catalogue. |
| **Delegated morality** | A1-12 Latour | Establishes that non-humans are actors in the social bond — artifacts delegate, prescribe, and shape conduct. "Frozen morality." |
| **Device paradigm** | A1-13 Borgmann | Establishes that modern culture is structured by the device paradigm (commodities available without the practices that give them meaning). Focal things and practices are the counter-concept. Feeds Anti-Extraction Audit. |
| **Field mapping** | A1-14 Mitcham | Establishes the engineering/humanities distinction as the map of the philosophy-of-technology field. A framing resource. |
| **Enframing origin-point** | A1-15 Heidegger | Establishes that the essence of technology is *Enframing* (Gestell) — a way of revealing that challenges forth and reduces everything to standing-reserve. The origin-point for the universality problem and the whole cosmotechnic response. |

## 2. The tissue: interconnecting links (fault lines that are also connections)

**2.1 The Simondon–Stiegler nexus (genesis + exteriorization)**
Simondon's concretization thesis (A1-05) and Stiegler's exteriorization thesis (A1-07) are complementary specializations: Simondon explains the *internal* genesis of technical objects; Stiegler explains the *human* side of the same process (memory exteriorized through prosthesis). Together they say: technical objects have an internal logic (Simondon), and this logic is the exteriorization of the human (Stiegler). The margin of indeterminacy (Simondon) is the space where the pharmacological effect (Stiegler) operates. *Design consequence:* the tool must preserve the margin of indeterminacy through which the human interprets — and this interpretation is always already pharmacological.

**2.2 The Hui–Heidegger nexus (cosmotechnics responds to enframing)**
Hui's cosmotechnics (A1-01/02/03) is explicitly a response to Heidegger's enframing (A1-15). Heidegger says: modern technology is Gestell — everything becomes standing-reserve. Hui says: but there is only *one* modern technology because we have only *one* (universal) technics; if we reopen the question of technics to plural cosmotechnics, we can resist enframing without nostalgia. *Design consequence:* the tool must answer to a cosmotechnic criterion internal to its milieu, not a universal "best practice."

**2.3 The Feenberg–Ellul nexus (democratic possibility vs. autonomous tendency)**
Feenberg's democratic rationalization (A1-08) and Ellul's autonomous technique (A1-17) are in direct tension. If technique is fully autonomous (Ellul), democratic participation in design is impossible. If democratic participation is possible (Feenberg), technique is not fully autonomous. *Resolution:* technique has *autonomous tendencies* that can be *resisted* but never fully overcome. Ellul is the warning; Feenberg is the possibility. The truth is between them. *Design consequence:* design for democratic participation while assuming that technique will tend to escape that participation — build in reversibility (S10) as a guard against closure.

**2.4 The Verbeek–Winner nexus (mediation + inscription)**
Verbeek's moral mediation (A1-10) and Winner's inscribed politics (A1-11) are complementary: Verbeek asks *what the tool does to moral experience* (process); Winner asks *what politics the tool settles* (outcome). A tool that mediates morally can still inscribe bad politics. *Design consequence:* apply both criteria — the tool's moral mediation (Verbeek) AND its political inscription (Winner).

**2.5 The Borgmann–Simondon nexus (focal practices + individuation)**
Borgmann's focal practices (A1-13) are the *stabilizations* that Simondon's individuation (A1-06) temporarily produces. Focal practices are the engagement that individuation crystallizes; individuation is the process that focal practices stabilize. *Design consequence:* the tool should support the user's becoming-through-practice (Simondon) by providing focal practices that center and order life (Borgmann).

**2.6 The Ihde–Hui nexus (universal grammar + plural content)**
Ihde's four relation-types (A1-09) are a *formal* taxonomy that applies across cultures; Hui's cosmotechnics (A1-01/02/03) is a *substantive* claim about the content of cosmos-morality-technics unifications. The same formal relation-type (e.g., embodiment) can mediate different cosmotechnic contents. *Design consequence:* the cosmotechnic audit must check both the formal relation-type (Ihde) and the substantive cosmotechnic content (Hui).

**2.7 The Haraway–Latour nexus (boundary breakdown + social bond)**
Haraway's cyborg (A1-18) is the *figure* of boundary breakdown; Latour's missing masses (A1-12) is the *mechanism* by which non-humans enter the social bond. Together they say: the boundaries are broken down (Haraway), and the non-humans that cross them are actors in the social bond (Latour). *Design consequence:* the tool is an actor in the moral bond, not a neutral instrument — and the boundaries between human and tool are permeable.

## 3. The living body: cross-cutting themes

**3.1 The pharmacological through-line**
Stiegler's *pharmakon* (A1-07) is the master-metaphor that runs through the whole spine. Every author, in some way, is describing the *dose* at which technics constitutes or dissolves the human:
- Simondon: the margin of indeterminacy is the *dose* of openness vs. closure
- Feenberg: the indeterminist margin is the *dose* of political openness vs. technical closure
- Heidegger: enframing is the *dose* of standing-reserve vs. bringing-forth
- Verbeek: moral mediation is the *dose* of moral agency vs. moral dissolution
- Borgmann: the device paradigm is the *dose* of convenience vs. engagement
- Ellul: autonomous technique is the *dose* of human direction vs. technical self-augmentation

The cosmotechnic audit must assess *dose* — not whether a tool is "good" or "bad" in itself, but what dose of exteriorization/mediation/inscription it produces.

**3.2 The genesis-process through-line**
Simondon's *genesis* (A1-05/06) is the master-process that runs through the whole spine. Every author is describing some dimension of the *coming-into-being* of the human-technical relation:
- Stiegler: the genesis of the human through exteriorization
- Ihde: the genesis of perception through technological mediation
- Verbeek: the genesis of moral experience through moral mediation
- Borgmann: the genesis of meaning through focal practice vs. device
- Haraway: the genesis of the cyborg through boundary breakdown

The cosmotechnic audit must assess *genesis* — how the tool brings the human-technical relation into being, and whether that genesis is open or closed.

**3.3 The politics-ethics through-line**
Winner's *politics* (A1-11) and Verbeek's *ethics* (A1-10) are the two dimensions of the normative question. Politics asks: *who decides?* Ethics asks: *what is good?* The two are inseparable:
- Feenberg: democratic rationalization is both a political and an ethical requirement
- Ellul: autonomous technique is both a political threat (to democracy) and an ethical threat (to human flourishing)
- Haraway: boundary breakdown is both a political possibility (new coalitions) and an ethical question (what unities to preserve)

The cosmotechnic audit must assess both *politics* (who decides?) and *ethics* (what is good?).
