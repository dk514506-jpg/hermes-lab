## Framework: Theoretical Domains Framework (TDF)

- **Canonical sources**:
  - Michie S, Johnston M, Abraham C, Lawton R, Parker D, Walker A (on behalf of the 'Psychological Theory' Group), "Making psychological theory useful for implementing evidence based practice: a consensus approach," *Qual Saf Health Care* 14:26–33, 2005 — **VERIFIED** (PubMed abstract fetched; BMJ full-text PDF blocked by Cloudflare, so body claims beyond the abstract are flagged below).
  - Cane J, O'Connor D, Michie S, "Validation of the theoretical domains framework for use in behaviour change and implementation research," *Implementation Science* 7:37, 2012 — **VERIFIED** (full text fetched and read).
  - Zhou Y et al., "Theoretical Domains Framework: A Bibliometric and Visualization Analysis from 2005–2023," *J Multidiscip Healthc* 17:4055–4069, 2024 — **VERIFIED** (full text fetched). Wu X et al., Front Digit Health 6:1362395, 2024 — **VERIFIED** (post-2020 application paper, full text fetched).

- **Core constructs** (14 domains, Cane 2012; construct counts in parens): **Knowledge** (3) — procedural/scientific knowledge; **Skills** (7) — competence in performing behaviour; **Social/Professional Role and Identity** (9) — self-concept, role obligations; **Beliefs about Capabilities** (8) — self-efficacy, perceived competence; **Optimism** (4) — dispositional optimism/pessimism; **Beliefs about Consequences** (5) — anticipated outcomes and their valuation; **Reinforcement** (7) — contingencies, rewards, associative learning; **Intentions** (3) — conscious resolve to act; **Goals** (6) — target end-states; **Memory, Attention and Decision Processes** (5) — cognitive load, attentional control; **Environmental Context and Resources** (6) — material/social context, resources; **Social Influences** (11) — norms, pressure, modelling; **Emotions** (7) — affective states; **Behavioural Regulation** (3) — self-monitoring, action planning. Total: 84 component constructs.

- **Claimed mechanism**: TDF is an integrative, consensus-derived synthesis of 33 behaviour-change theories and 128 key constructs (Michie 2005, via Cane 2012 recounting), whose purpose is *theoretically assessing an implementation problem before designing an intervention*: interview/focus-group/ questionnaire data are coded to domains to locate barriers and facilitators, so intervention design targets the right influences rather than intuition. Cane 2012 claims three advantages: comprehensive coverage of influences, clarity via component constructs, and links between behaviour-change theories and techniques. The 14 domains map onto the COM-B system (Capability/Opportunity/Motivation) with 100% inter-expert agreement in a 3-expert mapping (Cane 2012, Table 3).

- **Signature methods/instruments**: expert card-sort validation (open sort + Fuzzy Cluster Analysis; closed sort + Discriminant Content Validation; 37 experts, 112 unique constructs — Cane 2012); TDF-informed semi-structured interview guides/focus groups and questionnaires (Cane 2012; Michie 2005 phase 6 "piloting interview questions"); barrier/facilitator coding to domains; COM-B linkage for intervention targeting; a 4-step theory-informed intervention-development approach (French et al. 2012) and a practical user guide (Atkins et al. 2017) — both cited in Zhou 2024 (**VERIFIED as cited, papers not fetched**).

- **Evidence landmarks**: (1) Davies et al. 2010 — only 22.5% of 235 guideline-implementation studies used behaviour-change theory (cited in Cane 2012) — the problem TDF addresses. (2) Cane 2012: 13-cluster solution was optimal (average silhouette 0.29); 14-domain refinement; 6 constructs allocated to >1 domain (e.g., Action planning → Goals + Behavioural Regulation). (3) Zhou 2024: 1382 WoS publications 2005–2023, accelerating from ~10/yr (2012) to 301 (2023); England/Australia/Canada lead; Cane 2012 is the field's most-cited TDF paper (2167 citations); qualitative interview/focus-group studies predominate. (4) Wu 2024: TDF+COM-B study of 40 clinicians found 19 barriers/7 enablers to internet-hospital use; all 14 domains implicated, motivation most frequently cited. **Contrary findings (flagged)**: Cane 2012's own data are weak by its stated thresholds — average silhouette 0.29 is below the 0.50 "weak structure" cutoff; 'Knowledge' did not form an independent cluster in the open sort, and Cane 2012 notes knowledge is not an important source of variance for most health behaviours (citing Kenkel 1991 et al.); 'Environmental Context and Resources', 'Behavioural Regulation', and 'Memory, Attention and Decision Processes' clustered weakly and were retained only on closed-sort confidence ratings.

- **Tensions/open questions**: **12-domain vs 14-domain witness conflict — preserved, not harmonized.** Michie 2005 reports 12 domains (with 'Nature of the Behaviours' and 'emotion regulation'); Cane 2012 reports 14 (split 'Motivation and Goals' → Intentions/Goals; 'Beliefs about Consequences' → Beliefs about Consequences/Reinforcement; 'Beliefs about Capabilities' → Beliefs about Capabilities/Optimism; dropped 'Nature of the Behaviours'; 'Emotion' → 'Emotions'). Cane 2012 explicitly frames itself as "an advance on the 2005 version"; Zhou 2024 recounts the 12→14 history. Both are valid witnesses; an agent should encode version as metadata. Minor secondary witness discrepancy: Cane 2012 says Michie 2005 involved "16 health service researchers"; PubMed's Michie abstract says 13. Other tensions: domain overlap is acknowledged and *formalized* (fuzzy memberships, 6 dual-allocated constructs); TDF is a framework, not a process theory — it specifies no relations among domains (**RECONSTRUCTED inference**); validity rests on a single 37-participant expert sort; scope limited to constructs present in the original framework (Cane 2012, stated limitation).

- **Agent-operability notes**: TDF is directly graph-operable: 14 domain nodes + 84 construct nodes as a two-level ontology; utterance→construct→domain classification as typed edges with confidence scores (silhouette/ICC values from Cane 2012 as provenance weights); multi-domain allocations as weighted edges (e.g., 'Beliefs' → Beliefs about Consequences + Beliefs about Capabilities); COM-B as a compression layer (14→3 nodes); barrier/facilitator grid = aggregate counts and salience per domain from coded user statements, feeding intervention-function selection.

- **Home-lab relevance**: a personal-development agent can run TDF as a *diagnostic front-end*: parse user statements about a stuck habit/goal into a 14-domain barrier/facilitator grid, then triage (e.g., capability vs motivation vs opportunity) before suggesting actions — mirroring Wu 2024's clinician workflow at single-user scale.

## Framework: Implementation Science (NPT + realist evaluation lenses)

- **Canonical sources**:
  - May C, Finch T, "Implementing, Embedding, and Integrating Practices: An Outline of Normalization Process Theory," *Sociology* 43(3):535–554, 2009 — **VERIFIED** (full text fetched via UCSF-hosted copy).
  - Pawson R, Tilley N, *Realistic Evaluation*, Sage, 1997 — **UNVERIFIED** (book not fetched; paywalled). Its claims are characterized here only through **VERIFIED secondary sources**: betterevaluation.org overview; TASO realist-evaluation briefing; and a Pawson & Tilley 2004 chapter PDF (cnxus.org).
  - Lacouture A et al., "The concept of mechanism from a realist approach: a scoping review," *Nurs Res Pract* 2015 — **UNVERIFIED** (identified via search only; relevant as a critique landmark).

- **Core constructs**: NPT (May & Finch 2009) — implementation is *work*: "purposive social action that involves the investment of personal and group resources to achieve goals," operationalized through **four generative mechanisms**: (1) **Coherence** — work defining/organizing the practice as a meaningful cognitive-behavioural ensemble (components: differentiation, individual/communal specification, internalization; investment: meaning); (2) **Cognitive participation** — enrolment and engagement of actors (initiation, legitimation, enrolment, activation; investment: commitment); (3) **Collective action** — work operationalizing the practice (immediate: interactional workability, relational integration; organizing: skill-set workability, contextual integration; investment: effort); (4) **Reflexive monitoring** — formal/informal appraisal (systematization, individual/communal appraisal, reconfiguration; investment: comprehension). Realist evaluation (Pawson & Tilley 1997) — **context–mechanism–outcome (CMO) configurations**: mechanisms are unobservable generative processes that "fire" only in conducive contexts; the guiding question is "what works, in which circumstances, for whom?", and evaluation is a theory-testing loop: initial programme theory → CMO propositions → refinement.

- **Claimed mechanism**: NPT claims embedding/normalization of a practice is produced and reproduced by actors' continuous work in interaction chains; "individual intention and preferences are thus necessary, but not sufficient, explanations" — a direct counter-position to intention-centric psychology (May & Finch 2009; they cite meta-analytic evidence that intention theories explain up to ~25% of outcome variance — Webb & Sheeran 2006 — and critiques by Ogden 2003, Noar & Zimmerman 2005, Weinstein 2007). Realist evaluation claims outcomes are generated, not caused linearly: "it is not programmes which work but rather people who co-operate and choose to make them work" (Pawson & Tilley 1997: 58, quoted in TASO briefing); mechanisms are context-sensitive, and the same intervention fires different mechanisms in different participants.

- **Signature methods/instruments**: NPT-style process evaluation tracing the four mechanisms longitudinally (exemplar: teledermatology case in May & Finch 2009); later operationalized instruments such as NoMAD (**UNVERIFIED** — identified but not fetched); realist evaluation cycle — elicit programme theory, collect data on C×M→O patterns, adjudicate the most robust CMO configuration, revise theory (betterevaluation.org, VERIFIED); Pawson & Tilley's policy-question table showing realist answers to "did it work?" are always conditional ("It depends," "parts only, in some places and at some times" — cnxus chapter, VERIFIED).

- **Evidence landmarks**: (1) May & Finch 2009 — NPT's propositional scheme (mechanism-defining, component-defining, investment-defining propositions) and the authors' own caveat that the tabular form implies "a degree of rigidity that is completely alien to the phenomena"; (2) within-paper evidence that *informal* appraisal modes ("clinical experience") associate with embedding, while highly formal RCT-style evaluation associates with integration problems (Finch et al. 2007; May 2006a, cited in May & Finch 2009); (3) realist evaluation's widespread uptake in health/social policy (documented across the verified secondary sources); (4) Lacouture 2015 scoping review flagging ambiguity in the mechanism concept (**UNVERIFIED** — cited as an identified critique only). **Contrary findings (flagged)**: NPT's own authors concede prediction is an open problem; realist mechanism/context boundary disputes are a known literature tension.

- **Tensions/open questions**: NPT formalism vs process fluidity (authors' own caveat, VERIFIED); whether "work" framing under-specifies power relations (May & Finch acknowledge "the play of power" but do not theorize it — **RECONSTRUCTED** reading of VERIFIED text); realist evaluation's mechanisms are unobservable by definition, making falsification ambiguous (Pawson & Tilley via TASO; boundary disputes via Lacouture flag); disciplinary tension with TDF: NPT treats individual-intention models as insufficient, whereas TDF is built substantially from them — the two lenses answer different questions (individual diagnosis vs collective embedding).

- **Agent-operability notes**: NPT gives four longitudinal **process-state nodes** (coherence → cognitive participation → collective action → reflexive monitoring, with reconfiguration feeding back into coherence) — an agent can track a habit or tool's "normalization state" over time and diagnose *where the work is stalling*; each mechanism's components are atomic sub-operations. Realist evaluation gives **CMO triples as typed edges** (Context → [Mechanism] → Outcome): the agent forms conditional hypotheses ("in context C, mechanism M fires for user U → outcome O") and revises them on observed outcomes — a built-in "no universal intervention" guard.

- **Home-lab relevance**: NPT reframes personal-development goals as *embedding work* — a new routine is normalized only when users (and their environments) invest meaning, commitment, effort, and appraisal; the agent can surface which investment type is missing. Realist logic personalizes the Valens-style discipline: what worked once for this user in this context is a CMO hypothesis, not a law.

- **Source fidelity flags**: May & Finch 2009 = VERIFIED (full text); Pawson & Tilley 1997 = UNVERIFIED (book), characterized via VERIFIED secondary sources (betterevaluation.org, TASO briefing, cnxus 2004 chapter); NoMAD instrument = UNVERIFIED; Lacouture 2015 = UNVERIFIED (search-identified); the "power under-theorized" reading = RECONSTRUCTED.

**Synthesis**: TDF is the *taxonomy* layer — a validated, graph-ready ontology of 14 domains/84 constructs for diagnosing individual-level barriers and facilitators and compressing them into COM-B triage; implementation science (NPT + realist evaluation) is the *process and logic* layer — NPT explains why change becomes (or fails to become) routine as collective embedding work, and realist evaluation supplies the conditional causal grammar (CMO) that refuses context-free "what works" claims. The highest-value transferable elements for the agent architecture are: (1) the 14-domain/84-construct coding scheme as a stable barrier/facilitator ontology with version metadata (12 vs 14), (2) the verified TDF→COM-B mapping as a compression edge to intervention targeting, (3) NPT's four mechanisms as longitudinal process-state nodes for tracking habit normalization, and (4) CMO triples as the explanatory loop that keeps every recommendation conditional on context — consistent with the campaign's core discipline that witness conflicts and context-dependence are preserved, never harmonized away.
