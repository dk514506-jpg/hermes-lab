## Area: LLM role-play and conversational training

- **Key papers 2025-2026:**
  - 2026 · Fang Z, Zhang Y, Huang Y. "AgentForge: An Immersive Role-Playing Platform for Learning Agentic Software Engineering." arXiv:2608.04148. **VERIFIED** (abstract fetched). Novices take one of four SE roles (Task Planner, Patch Author, Code Reviewer, Test Runner) inside a multi-agent repair workflow with role-based scaffolding and metacognitive support; N=37 novices completed tasks at high rates, and the Code Reviewer practice demanded the most turns and was hardest, yet participants reported significant gains in understanding repair and agent collaboration (p_adj<.001). Demonstrates that role-play is most effective when the trainee's role has visible coordination demands and intermediate artifacts.
  - 2026 · Shouqi S, Nazly A, Wanniarachchi J, De Alwis R. "Adversarial Stress Testing of Role-Playing Language Agents using Multi-Agent Evaluation." arXiv:2608.03166 (ADScAI 2026). **VERIFIED**. A three-agent platform (Interrogator, Target RPLA, automated Judge) applies six progressive adversarial strategies over multi-turn dialogue; multi-strategy testing revealed failure modes invisible to single-strategy testing, dropping robustness 0.17–0.20, with Authority Challenge and Emotional Manipulation most effective; judge aligned with humans (r=0.82, κ=0.71). Role-playing agents degrade cumulatively under pressure — a safety gate requirement for any deployed role-play partner.
  - 2025 · Rudolph E, Steigerwald P, Albrecht J. "Comparing Human Roleplayers and LLM-Simulated Clients in Online Counselling Training: An Analysis of Counselling Patterns." EDM 2025 (educationaldatamining.org/EDM2025/proceedings/2025.EDM.short-papers.116). **VERIFIED**. Compared 64 human–human vs 74 human–LLM (Mixtral 8x7B) counselling role-plays coded at sentence level with the GeCCo system; LLM clients replicated many conversational dynamics but produced measurable differences in how clients disclosed concerns and how counsellors structured sessions. First quantitative head-to-head of LLM vs human role-play partners in skill training; supports feasibility while quantifying the authenticity gap.
  - 2025 · Voigt H, Sugamiya Y, Lawonn K, Zarrieß S, Takanishi A. "LLM-Powered Virtual Patient Agents for Interactive Clinical Skills Training with Automated Feedback." arXiv:2508.13943. **VERIFIED**. Extends text-based LLM simulated patients with an action space (non-textual behaviors) plus virtual tutors giving real-time personalized feedback during OSCE-style encounters; expert evaluation of naturalness and feedback appropriateness. Architecture pattern: separate patient-simulation and feedback/tutor components.
  - 2025 (v3 2026) · Zhang B, Liu X, Wang Y, Zhou L, Xie Q, Wang B. "Human or LLM as Standardized Patients? A Comparative Study for Medical Education" (EasyMED + SPBench). arXiv:2511.14783. **VERIFIED**. Multi-agent virtual standardized patient that separates case-grounded information disclosure from response generation for stable, inquiry-conditioned behavior; in a 4-week controlled study, learning outcomes were comparable to human standardized patients with stronger early gains for novices and better psychological safety. Direct evidence that structured LLM role-play can match human role-players for training outcomes.
  - 2025 · Ma X, Zhu R, Wang Z, Xiong J, Chen Q, Tang H, Camp LJ, Ohno-Machado L. "Enhancing Patient-Centric Communication: Leveraging LLMs to Simulate Patient Perspectives." arXiv:2501.06964. **VERIFIED**. LLMs primed with educational-background personas gave accurate, actionable guidance 88% of the time, but adding other demographic information dropped performance below random chance, and accuracy was biased against underserved populations. Critical caution: persona-primed role-players can be less reliable than a plain query-response model.

  - 2024 · Louie R et al. ## Area: User modeling / behavior latticing / motivation inference

- **Key papers 2025-2026:**
  - 2026 · Phu AJ, Mooney J, de Langis K, Le KC, Kang D. "SERUM: State Extraction and Refinement for User Modeling." arXiv:2607.29181. **VERIFIED**. Extracts finite-state behavioral models (action + intent state vocabularies) directly from unstructured egocentric screen video via hierarchical VLM annotation; iterative refinement converges to a stable taxonomy ("schematic equilibrium") and fitted Markov models substantially beat frequency baselines at predicting next actions, with largest gains on structured tasks (coding). This is the closest operational evidence for a "behavior latticing" claim: interpretable state-lattice models of user behavior can be learned without manual annotation.
  - 2026 · Shaikh O, Teutschbein V, Gandhi K, Chi Y, Haber N, Robinson T, Ram N, Reeves B, Yang S, Bernstein MS, Yang D. "Learning Next Action Predictors from Human-Computer Interaction." arXiv:2603.05923. **VERIFIED**. Formalizes next-action prediction (NAP): 360K labeled actions across 1,800 hours of continuous phone use from 20 users; LongNAP (parametric + in-context retrieval of user-specific reasoning traces) beats supervised fine-tuning by 79% and prompted baselines by 39%, but only 17.1% of predicted trajectories align well with actual next actions (26% when filtered to high confidence). Proactive prediction is viable but far from solved — high-value, low-reliability signal.
  - 2026 · Liu A, Karoui Y, Draxler F, Kreuter F, Chiossi F. "Sensing What Surveys Miss: Understanding and Personalizing Proactive LLM Support by User Modeling." arXiv:2602.00880 (accepted CHI 2026). **VERIFIED**. Uses electrodermal activity + mouse movement to model when users need help; aligned-adaptive LLM assistance improved response accuracy 21% and cut false negatives from 50.9% to 22.9% versus misaligned/random timing (N=32). Evidence that timing support to inferred cognitive state matters more than content alone — supports the "readiness-sensitive intervention" design principle.
  - 2025 · Lim K, Jung Y-C, Kim B-H. "Evaluating motivational interview quality using large language models and hidden Markov models." BMC Psychiatry 25:908. DOI 10.1186/s12888-025-07391-1; PMID 41034852. **VERIFIED**. LLM-scored client utterances (change- vs sustain-talk valence) fed into HMMs over 40 MI sessions (AnnoMI corpus): high-quality sessions show fluid transitions between motivational states, low-quality sessions show persistence in resistance-oriented states (p<.001), and transition-matrix features predict session quality at 0.80 LOOCV accuracy. Direct empirical grounding for modeling motivation as a trackable latent state with transition dynamics — the psychological core of "behavior latticing."
  - 2026 · Di Y, Liu Y, Chen Z, Guo J. "SRSUPM: Sequential Recommender System Based on User Psychological Motivation." arXiv:2602.08667. **WITHDRAWN — flagged, NOT cited as evidence** (author withdrew v3 on 17 Apr 2026; comment: "The article contains experimental errors"). Noted here only to record that an explicit psychological-motivation-shift user-modeling framework existed in this window and was retracted; do not build on it without independent replication.

- **Key papers 2024 (pre-window, only if foundational):**
  - 2024 · Zheng Z, Chao W, Qiu Z, et al. "Harnessing Large Language Models for Text-Rich Sequential Recommendation." WWW '24. DOI 10.1145/3589334.3645358. **UNVERIFIED** (seed-registry entry, DOI documented; not independently fetched this session). LLM-based user modeling over text-rich interaction sequences; foundational for language-grounded user-state encoding in recommendation/behavior sequences.
  - 2023 · Wu Z et al. "AnnoMI: A Dataset of Expert-Motivational-Interviewing Annotated Transcripts." **RECONSTRUCTED** (dataset described in and used by verified Lim et al. 2025 and Mahmood et al. 2025; original not fetched). The standard expert-annotated corpus linking client language (change talk, sustain talk) to motivational state — foundational for motivation-inference-from-language work.

- **Convergent findings:** (1) Motivational/cognitive state is learnable and predictive: change-talk valence sequences and HMM transition dynamics predict session quality (Lim), and finite-state action/intent models predict user behavior (SERUM). (2) LLM/VLM annotation pipelines can now extract structured user models from raw behavioral streams (screen video, HCI logs) at scale — the "latticing" operation is becoming automatable. (3) Proactive interventions improve outcomes only when timed to inferred user state (Liu), reinforcing state-aware over content-only personalization.
- **Contrary/conflicting findings:** Prediction ceilings are low: only ~17-26% of next-action trajectories align with reality (Shaikh), so overconfident proactive behavior is a real risk. SRSUPM's withdrawal shows motivation-shift modeling is fragile under scrutiny. Ma et al. (Area 1) shows inferred-persona personalization can *hurt* accuracy versus unprimed baselines — personalization is not monotonically beneficial.
- **Gaps:** No longitudinal datasets linking inferred motivational state → intervention → behavior change across weeks/months; privacy constraints on egocentric/HCI user modeling are unsolved; no standard benchmark for "motivation inference" as a task (limbs of the field use MISC/MITI codes, recommender targets, or next-action accuracy — incomparable); field validation of SERUM-style extraction outside lab/screen domains.
- **Agent-architecture implications (RECONSTRUCTED):** A home-lab agent should (a) maintain an explicit motivational-state model (change-talk/sustain-talk valence, state-transition history) updated per interaction, mirroring Lim et al.; (b) derive its "behavior lattice" via SERUM-style iterative state extraction with a convergence criterion rather than one-pass classification; (c) treat next-action/intent predictions as low-confidence priors (17-26% alignment) used for timing, not for autonomous action; (d) gate proactive outreach on inferred readiness signals (physiological, behavioral, linguistic) instead of fixed schedules; (e) store per-user reasoning traces for in-context personalization (LongNAP pattern).
- **Source fidelity flags:** SRSUPM withdrawn for experimental errors (flagged, excluded). Two of five core entries are preprints (SERUM, NAP); one is conference-accepted (Liu, CHI 2026); Lim et al. is peer-reviewed (BMC Psychiatry).   - 2024 · Cohen A et al. "Using Large Language Models to Automate Counsellor and Client Coding in Motivational Interviewing." **RECONSTRUCTED** (as cited in verified Mahmood et al. 2025).   - 2024 · Steenstra I et al. Fully generative LLM virtual agent for alcohol counseling. **RECONSTRUCTED** (as cited in verified Mahmood et al. 2025). - **Convergent findings:** (1) AI/LLM delivery of MI is technically feasible and achieves *fidelity* comparable to or exceeding novice humans (Aimi, MIBot), especially when structured workflows or state-tracking orchestrate the conversation (Aimi, CALM-IT). (2) The bottleneck has shifted from fidelity to *durability*: readiness/confidence move in RCTs (Li; Eiroa-Solans; MIBot), but sustained behavior change is rarely demonstrated (3/15 studies in Karve scoping review; 24h decay in Intrinsic AI). (3) MI transfers across settings (justice, military, health, education) as a human-delivered method, but AI-delivered MI has only been tested in health behaviors (smoking, alcohol, vaccination, prosocial).

- **Agent-architecture implications (RECONSTRUCTED):** A motivational-ecology agent should (a) implement MI as *structured workflow with explicit state tracking* (change-talk/sustain-talk valence per turn; reflection-to-question ratio and complex-reflection share as runtime quality gates per Aimi's MISC-2 metrics); (b) favor evocation over prompting — CALM-IT's fewer-but-better-timed evocations with higher acceptance is the design target; (c) expect and design for readiness→behavior decay: schedule repeated, spaced MI-style sessions (Intrinsic AI's 24h decay) rather than one-shot interventions; (d) keep a human-in-the-loop for empathy-critical or crisis-adjacent turns, since perceived empathy is the consistent deficit; (e) use the justice-setting meta-analytic evidence (attendance, dropout, recidivism) to justify MI-style engagement in coercive/low-autonomy contexts, but preserve autonomy-supportive language there.

## Synthesis

Across all three areas, the 2025-2026 evidence forms a coherent, mutually-reinforcing loop: LLM role-play supplies scalable simulated interlocutors and practice environments with measurable learning outcomes; user modeling supplies the machinery to extract structured, evolving behavioral and motivational state ("lattices") from raw interaction data; and MI supplies the best-evidenced conversational method for moving that state, complete with fidelity metrics (MISC/MITI, change-talk ratios) that can now be computed automatically. The honest convergence, however, is a ceiling at *readiness, not behavior*: AI-delivered MI reliably shifts importance, confidence, and readiness in RCTs, yet durable behavior change is rare, empathy lags humans, and next-action/motivation prediction remains low-confidence. The architecture implication for the home-lab agent is to treat motivation as a tracked latent state that is latticed from evidence, moved by MI-faithful, state-timed dialogue with fidelity gates, trained and stress-tested through role-play, and evaluated on sustained behavior — with explicit humility gates wherever a single session or single prediction would otherwise overclaim.
