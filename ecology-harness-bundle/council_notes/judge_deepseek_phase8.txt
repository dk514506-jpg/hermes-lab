---

## 0. Verdict summary

This is a serious, mostly-rigorous evaluation design with three genuinely excellent properties —
criteria that are artifact-anchored with verifier guards, checklists that are honestly applied
(dozens of GAP/PARTIAL markers, no machine-coverage overclaiming), and a calibration log that
records real corrections with artifact line numbers. It is not yet fully self-consistent:
the single most damaging defect is that the master rubric's described "verifier chain (run at
every gate)" does not match the gate that actually runs (verify_all.py), and the Phase 8 verifier
itself is absent from the documented chain. There are also three design gaps a rigorous evaluator
would close: scores are not required to cite the artifact they rest on, there is no inter-rater
reliability protocol, and runtime-scoped claims are not explicitly carved out of a static-file
evaluation. Verdict: **revise once (targeted, not rebuild)** — the kernel is sound.

## 1. Do the 10 criteria DISCRIMINATE quality? — YES, with caveats

**Strong (evidence):**
- The 0/1/2 ladder (ABSENT/CONTRADICTED → PARTIAL/prose-only → FULL/structural+verifier-guarded)
  is a real quality gradient, not a checklist of existence. "2" requires structural encoding AND
  cross-artifact consistency AND a named verifier; "1" catches the prose-only case; "0" catches
  contradiction. A weak artifact scores 0 and a strong one scores 2 under every criterion.
- Numeric anchors make discrimination objective and checkable: C1's reference counts
  (COMB 14, TDF 10, PPS 9, MI 14, MLG 12, ConvoDojo 13, HEB 11) — I counted all seven from
  Phase3_Skills/*/atomic_ops.json and every number matches. C8's stage counts (Ambivalence 6,
  Conflict 6, Workplace 9) match the five dialogue_state_machine.json files exactly.
- The rubric is willing to score its own architecture down: C3 explicitly caps at 1 ("runtime
  EXECUTION of the gate is declaration + policy only"), C5 caps at 1 ("no runtime record"),
  C7 caps at 1 ("verdict→quarantine→log chain is not asserted"). A rubric that demotes its own
  headline claims on the evidence is discriminating in the right direction.

**Weak (specifics):**
- The generic ladder's "enforced by a named verifier (where one exists)" contains an escape
  hatch: a criterion with NO verifier can still reach 2 on structural grounds alone. The
  per-criterion texts mostly override this with explicit caps (C3/C5/C7), but the escape hatch
  remains in the master ladder and will be read literally by a careless rater.
- C1 is nearly saturated in a mature tree (fully verifier-guarded, all counts correct) — it will
  almost always score 2, so it discriminates little in practice. The rubric does not acknowledge
  ceiling effects or weight criteria; C3/C7/C9 (safety) count the same as C1 (bookkeeping).
- Mixed-level criteria are scored as one unit: C3 contains a declaration claim (2-capable) and a
  runtime claim (1-capped) inside one ladder. The text resolves it, but §1.3's "scored per
  artifact" instruction gives no rule for a criterion whose sub-claims sit at different levels —
  a rater must improvise.

## 2. Are the QA checklists ANSWERABLE from the artifacts they reference? — Mostly YES

**Strong (evidence):**
- Every row names an exact file path, a pass condition, and an enforcement status
  (verified-by-check-N vs GAP). This is the campaign's best property; it converts review from
  opinion into re-derivable audit.
- I independently re-ran the enforcement claims. All eight verifiers exit 0
  (verify_all, phase3, phase4, phase5, phase6, phase7, phase8, verify_critique_revisions,
  verify_packages). The "all three verifiers pass (verified 2026-08-06)" claim in the skill
  checklist is reproducible. verify_phase6 executes exactly 257 checks — the dojo checklist's
  "257 checks" claim is exact. verify_phase5 executes 21 ("21 checks + guards" — exact);
  verify_critique_revisions executes 20 ("20 checks" — exact).
- The GAP markers are real, not rhetorical: R3 (TDF SKILL.md still writes `binding_constraint:
  enum C|O|M|none` while state_schema.json renamed it `binding_constraint_comb`) — I confirmed
  the drift at TDF_Barrier_Facilitator_Grid/SKILL.md:44,59. R2 (HEB state_schema.json carries
  "Unification pending Phase 4" for the skill_load_score→trend conversion) — confirmed at
  Human_Empowerment_Boundary/state_schema.json:15. G1 (Conversation/Coaching declare
  `user_agreement_requirement`; Ambivalence/Conflict/Workplace use `user_agreement`) — confirmed
  across all five sparring_intensity_profile.json files. The checklists report the tree's real
  warts, which is what makes them answerable rather than promotional.

**Weak (specifics):**
- The rubric's §1.5 artifact map (evidence index) has path errors a rater will hit:
  it directs to `evidence/Recent_Evidence_Digest.md, Annotated_Bibliography.md,
  Contrary_Findings_and_Limits.md, phase2_api_seed.md` — but no `evidence/` directory exists in
  the source tree; those three files sit at the Foundation root, and phase2_api_seed.md lives in
  council_notes/ (the packaged copy exists at Hermes_Agent_Harness/evidence/). Similarly the
  "Foundation/" prefix on Phase 1 files is loose (they are at the Foundation root). The checklists
  themselves cite exact paths; the master rubric's index should match their standard.
- C6's "digest↔bib numeric fidelity (critique_0 traced ~60 cross-references — all matched)" cites
  an event, not a file: a rater cannot re-derive "~60" without redoing the whole trace. Either
  name the trace artifact or move the claim to Calibration_Log evidence.
- C5's top claim is honest but unanswerable today: "whether the runtime log actually records
  unassisted completions is unchecked" — the logs ship empty, so there is nothing to read. The
  rubric handles this via the cap-at-1, which is the right move; the residual issue is that the
  criterion still asks raters to score something no artifact can answer. It needs the
  runtime-deferred treatment proposed in Recommendation 5, not just a cap.
- The dojo checklist's G1 misses one fact I found: Phase6_Dojo/README.md:62 declares the
  convention "Intensity profiles declare `user_agreement.required: true`" — which two of the five
  profiles violate. The verifier drift is caught; the README-vs-profiles convention drift is not.
- The 78-entry bibliography count is correct (I confirmed 78 numbered entries; the 79th `### [`
  match is the format template in the Entry Format section), and the Status-first-field format is
  real. No issue there.

## 3. Is the scoring/aggregation protocol sound? — Sound shape, three ambiguities

**Strong:**
- Median-of-rater-scores with rounding, disagreement-triggered reconciliation into
  Calibration_Log, PASS/WARN/FAIL bands on the mean, and a per-artifact floor (any 0 blocks that
  artifact's PASS; any 0-scoring artifact blocks upload per §3) — the pieces are coherent and the
  floor is the sharpest guard in the design. Band math checks out: PASS ≥1.8 on a 0–2 mean means
  at least 8 of 10 criteria at 2 with the rest at 1; FAIL <1.2 means mostly-ones — both are
  meaningful thresholds, not decorative.

**Weak (specifics):**
- Rounding rule is lenient at the boundary: median 1.5 (e.g., {1,1,2,2}) rounds to 2, so a
  criterion half the raters judged partial scores as full. The protocol does not say how to
  break 1.5 (I recommend: resolve toward the Council owner's score, or toward the lower score).
- Reconciliation is only triggered Council-vs-judge ("disagreement of ≥1 point between Council
  and a judge"); judge-vs-judge disagreement of a full point forces nothing. And the protocol
  does not say whether a reconciliation that vindicates the judge replaces the median or merely
  annotates it.
- No independence or reliability controls: nothing requires raters to score before seeing each
  other's scores, no agreement statistic (exact-agreement %, kappa) is computed, and no published
  score matrix makes the median auditable. §1.3 defines the math but not the record of it.
- Scale mismatch with the whole-project instrument: review_rubric.md (which the same outside
  judges apply) uses 0–5 per dimension with a mandatory one-line artifact citation per score;
  the Phase 8 master rubric uses 0–2 with NO citation requirement. A judge scoring the same
  campaign under both instruments is held to two different evidence standards. The 0–2 scale also
  gives only ~0.2 mean resolution — adequate for PASS/WARN/FAIL, weak for "discriminate quality"
  claims at the margin.
- §1.3 line 41 says criteria are scored "per artifact," but every criterion names multiple
  artifacts (C1 names five). The unit of scoring is ambiguous: one holistic score per criterion,
  or one score per named artifact aggregated? The math (§1.3) implies one score per criterion;
  the wording should say so.

## 4. Is the Calibration_Log complete and honest? — Largely yes; two self-description defects

**Strong (evidence):**
- Sixteen rows of finding→fix→verifier-guard, artifact-referenced at line-number granularity
  (README:33-34, Construct_Map:75, interface:55). Rows 9–16 show real outside-judge integration
  (DeepSeek R1–R5, Claude, DeepSeek 2.7) with the verifier that now holds each fix — matching
  review-rubric E4.
- Standing items Q6–Q11 are openly OPEN, including Q10, which indicts the campaign itself
  ("is Dallas deskilled by the councils that read for him?"). The log's honesty principle
  ("a correction that has no verifier guard is one revision away from regressing") is stated and
  mostly honored.
- The verifier register's counts check out where I measured them (verify_phase6 257, verify_phase5
  21, verify_critique_revisions 20). Minor drift: verify_phase4 is listed as "32 checks" but
  executes 35 — ironic for a log whose row 15 polices exactly this class of count drift.

**Weak (specifics):**
- **verify_all.py overclaim (the biggest defect in the package).** Calibration_Log's verifier
  register says verify_all.py "Chains every build-time verifier; exit 0 gate before upload," and
  Evaluation_Rubric.md §3 repeats it ("Verifier chain (run at every gate): verify_all.py →
  verify_phase3.py, verify_phase4.py, verify_phase5.py, verify_phase6.py, verify_phase7.py,
  verify_critique_revisions.py, verify_packages.py, _verify_skills.py + verify_harness.py. All
  must exit 0"). I read council_notes/verify_all.py: its docstring says "Single-entry
  verification for the Phase 4 changed paths" and it chains exactly three things —
  verify_packages.py, verify_critique_revisions.py, verify_phase4.py (lines 71–74) — plus two
  JSON parses. verify_phase5/6/7, _verify_skills.py, and verify_harness.py never run under it.
  (verify_phase4.py and verify_critique_revisions.py each chain verify_phase3.py, so phase 3 is
  reachable; phases 5/6/7 and the shipped harness verifier are not.) A gate that claims to run
  nine verifiers but runs three would pass with verify_phase6.py broken. This is a B3
  self-description inaccuracy of exactly the class the campaign polices everywhere else, in the
  document whose entire job is to police it.
- **verify_phase8.py is invisible to the design.** council_notes/verify_phase8.py exists, runs,
  and exits 0 (58 executed checks), but it appears in neither the rubric §3 chain nor the
  Calibration_Log verifier register — the evaluation's own verifier is missing from the
  evaluation's own documentation of its verifiers.
- **Residual row-1 drift:** row 1 says the artifact-level status was relabeled "assembled from
  VERIFIED council distillations (synthesis = RECONSTRUCTED)". Annotated_Bibliography.md:5 — in
  both the Foundation root copy and the packaged Hermes_Agent_Harness/evidence/ copy (I diffed;
  headers identical) — still reads "Status: VERIFIED — entries from council distillations
  (70+ sources fetched)" with no synthesis qualifier. The log's fix may have targeted the README
  wording only; if so, the bibliography's file-level "Status: VERIFIED" header is precisely the
  class of file-level header row 1 says was misread as content-verified. Worth a one-line check.
- **Phase 8's own findings are not yet in the log.** §1.4 and the Feed-forward section say Phase 8
  findings append as rows #17+, but the Council's own findings (R2/R3, G1–G7, the §1.5 path
  errors, the verify_all.py overclaim above) currently live only in checklist prose. The
  calibration phase has not yet calibrated itself.

## 5. What is MISSING that a rigorous evaluator would add?

- **A per-rater score matrix with mandatory artifact citations.** Nothing in §1.3 requires a
  rater to justify a 0/1/2 with the artifact it rests on. The whole-project rubric demands
  citations per score; the Phase 8 master rubric does not. Without the matrix, the median is a
  claim, not an auditable record.
- **Inter-rater reliability controls:** independent scoring before any discussion, an agreement
  statistic, and an adjudication path for judge-vs-judge full-point splits (the current
  reconciliation rule only covers Council-vs-judge).
- **A falsification/calibration set for the ladder:** the rubric claims to discriminate; nothing
  tests it. A 15-minute exercise — two synthetic artifacts, one deliberately weak (prose-only
  empowerment boundary, unanchored rubric) and one deliberately strong — rated by Council +
  judges would empirically validate the ladder and expose rater drift before live use.
- **Explicit runtime-deferral scoping.** The whole evaluation is static-file (logs ship empty;
  the lattice is SEED; C5's runtime claim is uncheckable). The design should state, in one
  annex, what is NOT evaluated (log conformance I2, skill_load_snapshot recording, verdict→
  quarantine execution, boundary-gate runtime blocking, actual T1–T6 firing) and the trigger for
  re-evaluation (first N live sessions / first real dojo run), so schema-level 2s are never
  mistaken for operational proof.
- **A sampling protocol for human-judgment criteria.** C6's "per-citation correctness is a
  human/judge sampling task" names no strata, no sample size, no stopping rule. Specify it
  (e.g., N citations per package, stratified by VERIFIED/RECONSTRUCTED/opinion/preprint).
- Secondary: an N/A rule (criteria not applicable to an artifact — the sibling checklists
  already map criteria to layers, so N/A arises in practice and the protocol is silent); a
  re-evaluation trigger so the point-in-time snapshot doesn't silently age.

## 6. Five concrete recommendations (priority-ordered)

1. **Make the gate match the description.** Rewrite council_notes/verify_all.py (or replace it
   with a runner) so it actually executes verify_phase3/4/5/6/7/8, verify_critique_revisions.py,
   verify_packages.py, _verify_skills.py, and Hermes_Agent_Harness/verify/verify_harness.py and
   fails on any nonzero exit. Then correct Evaluation_Rubric.md §3 and Calibration_Log's verifier
   register to name the real chain (including verify_phase8.py). This is a truthfulness defect in
   the exact class the campaign polices; it should be Calibration_Log row #17 with the guard being
   the repaired verify_all.py itself.

2. **Add the auditable score record + citation discipline.** Require every 0/1/2 score to carry a
   one-line artifact citation (mirror review_rubric.md's per-dimension format); publish a rater ×
   criterion matrix (Council A/B/C + each judge) with the median and each reconciliation note in
   Calibration_Log (or a Phase8_Scorecard); define the N/A rule and the 1.5 rounding rule
   (recommend: resolve toward the Council owner's score or the lower score).

3. **Log the Phase 8 Council's own findings as rows #17+.** G1–G7 (dojo), R2/R3 (state-schema
   drift), the §1.5 artifact-map path errors (`evidence/` does not exist in the source tree;
   phase2_api_seed.md is in council_notes/), the Phase6_Dojo/README.md:62 `user_agreement.required`
   convention drift (two profiles violate it), and the verify_all.py overclaim — each as
   finding → fix → verifier guard per §1.4, so the calibration phase calibrates itself before
   the campaign is declared complete.

4. **Validate the ladder empirically + fix the scale plumbing.** Run the synthetic weak/strong
   calibration exercise (Section 5) and record the agreement; add the sampling protocol for
   citation checks; split mixed-level criteria (C3, C5, C7) into separately-scored
   declaration-vs-runtime sub-claims so the cap-at-1 logic is mechanical, not interpretive; and
   reconcile the 0–2 (Phase 8) vs 0–5 (whole-project) scale mismatch in the judge briefs.

5. **Add the runtime-deferral annex.** One section (or a sixth evaluation artifact) listing what
   static evaluation cannot verify, which criteria are consequently capped and why, and the
   explicit trigger for re-evaluation after live use begins (e.g., after the first N logged dojo
   sessions). Wire it to Q8/Q11 in the Calibration_Log so the standing items carry the
   re-evaluation trigger rather than an open-ended "OPEN."
