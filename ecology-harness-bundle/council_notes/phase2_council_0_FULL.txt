## Area: Skill atrophy from AI assistance

- **Key papers 2025-2026:**
  - 2025, Budzyń K, Romańczyk M, Kitala D, et al. (incl. Bretthauer M, Mori Y). "Endoscopist deskilling risk after exposure to artificial intelligence in colonoscopy: a multicentre, observational study." *Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology* 10(10):896-903. DOI 10.1016/S2468-1253(25)00133-5. VERIFIED (PubMed, PMID 40816301; an author reply at 10.1016/S2468-1253(25)00324-3 is also verified, indicating active methodological debate). Endoscopists reverted to non-AI colonoscopy after sustained AI use showed a drop in adenoma detection rate (28.4%→22.4%) while it stayed stable with AI (25.3%) — figures as synthesized by Heudel et al. (below); the first large multi-centre quantitative signal of AI-exposure deskilling in a procedural specialty.
  - 2026, Heudel PE, Crochet H, Filori Q, Bachelot T, Blay JY. "Artificial intelligence in medicine: a scoping review of the risk of deskilling and loss of expertise among physicians." *ESMO Real World Data & Digital Oncology* 12:100693. DOI 10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100693. VERIFIED (full abstract). Concludes deskilling evidence is "scarce but consistent" across specialties: automation bias confirmed; erroneous AI prompts raised false-positive recalls up to 12% in breast radiologists; >30% of pathologists reversed correct diagnoses under time pressure; structural deskilling via 80-85% case-volume collapse in cytology.
  - 2025, Natali C, Marconi L, Dias Duran LD, et al. "AI-induced Deskilling in Medicine: A Mixed-Method Review and Research Agenda for Healthcare and Beyond." *Artificial Intelligence Review* 58:356. DOI 10.1007/s10462-025-11352-1. VERIFIED (open access, full text). Distinguishes *deskilling* (erosion of acquired skill) from *upskilling inhibition* (blocked acquisition, especially trainees); maps vulnerabilities (physical exam, differential diagnosis, judgment, communication) onto PACES competencies; proposes longitudinal monitoring and real-time impact tracking as the research agenda.
  - 2025, Lee HP, Sarkar A, Tankelevitch L, Drosos I, Rintel S, Banks R, Wilson N. "The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers." *CHI '25*. DOI 10.1145/3706598.3713778. VERIFIED (ACM full text). Survey of 319 knowledge workers: higher confidence in GenAI predicts *less* critical-thinking enaction and effort; effort shifts from task execution to verification/stewardship; over-reliance is the dominant risk pattern.
  - 2025, Bastani H, Bastani O, Sungu A, Ge H, Kabakcı Ö, Mariman R. "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics." *PNAS* 122(26):e2422633122. DOI 10.1073/pnas.2422633122. VERIFIED (full text). RCT, ~1,000 students: GPT-4 access raised practice performance (+48% grades) but when access was removed students scored 17% *worse* than never-treated controls; a hint-based "GPT Tutor" guardrail eliminated the harm; students were metacognitively blind to the loss.
  - 2025, Berzin TM, Topol EJ. "Preserving clinical skills in the age of AI assistance." *Lancet* 406(10513):1719. DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(25)02075-6. VERIFIED (PubMed, PMID 41109709). Commentary demanding skill-preservation mandates built into AI clinical deployment.
  - 2026, Gutoreva A, Tsim F, Papakonstantinou T. "Position: AI as Part of Self — Extending the Mind Requires Cognitive Co-Regulation." arXiv:2605.16197. VERIFIED. Names the risk cluster of unstructured delegation (deskilling, automation bias, epistemic-authority transfer, oracle centralization); argues alignment must target the human-AI *system* (co-regulation), since AI operates pre-consciously ("System 0").
- **Key papers 2024 (pre-window, foundational):**
  - 2024, Choudhury A, Chaudhry Z. "Large Language Models and User Trust: Consequence of Self-Referential Learning Loop and the Deskilling of Health Care Professionals." *J Med Internet Res* 26:e56764. DOI 10.2196/56764. VERIFIED. Theoretical: AI-generated data feeding AI training (self-referential loop) plus clinician deskilling; expert oversight as mitigation — the framing Natali et al. later systematized.
- **Convergent findings:** Assisted performance rises while independent post-assistance performance falls (Bastani, Budzyń); automation bias scales with trust/confidence in the tool (Lee, Heudel); skill loss is invisible to the user (Bastani's perception data; Lee's conflation of effort-with-support and effort-with-thinking); guardrail/friction design (hints vs. answers, oversight roles) is the decisive moderator (Bastani, Choudhury, Gutoreva); effects are heterogeneous — experience does not protect (Heudel's radiologist data).
- **Contrary/conflicting findings:** Quantitative deskilling evidence remains "scarce," small-N, observational or short-horizon (Heudel); group-level productivity studies (Brynjolfsson, QJE 2025 — see Area 2) show large gains with no measured skill loss, so performance gain and capability erosion can coexist without contradiction but are rarely measured in the same study; Budzyń's design and interpretation drew published author-reply debate; the CHI study is self-report, and authors note perceived effort reductions may reflect genuine support rather than atrophy.
- **Gaps:** No longitudinal (multi-year) measurement of expert skill trajectories under sustained AI assistance; no standardized "skill health" metric; no reversal/relearning curves after atrophy; no studies isolating *agent-initiated* (proactive) assistance from user-initiated assistance on skill outcomes; objective (non-self-report) telemetry of skill is essentially absent.
- **Agent-architecture implications:** Encode *minimal sufficiency* — deliver hints/scaffolds, not finished answers (Bastani's Tutor arm); maintain an unassisted-competence track per user and detect reliance creep (rising assistance fraction with falling independent accuracy); deliberately inject calibrated friction rather than optimizing zero-friction UX; treat user self-reports as unreliable and gate on objective performance telemetry; log "skill load" (share of tasks completed unassisted) as a first-class metric.

## Area: Empowerment-based assistive agents

- **Key papers 2025-2026:**
  - 2025, Brynjolfsson E, Li D, Raymond L. "Generative AI at Work." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 140(2):889-949. DOI 10.1093/qje/qjae044. VERIFIED (OUP landing page + identical NBER w31161 abstract). RCT on 5,179 customer-support agents: +14% average productivity (issues/hour); +34% for novice/low-skilled workers; minimal for experienced/high-skilled. Assistance compresses the skill gradient — empowerment is unevenly distributed and largest where capability is lowest.
  - 2026, Beacock I, Xu R, Murray L, Anson P, Goldberg B, Kumar D, Lee J, Park R, Sinha A. "AI usage patterns are shaped by perceived gains in human agency." arXiv:2607.02313. VERIFIED (full abstract). Deep ethnography (n=51, US/DE/SG): sustained conversational-AI use is driven by *perceived* agency gains that outweigh accuracy concerns, challenging trust-based adoption models; flags the critical tension between immediate psychological agency boosts and material/structural empowerment or long-term capacity.
  - 2026, Wang Y, et al. "User Autonomy in Human-LLM Interaction: A Scoping Review." *CHI EA '26* (Barcelona). DOI 10.1145/3772363.3798855. VERIFIED (ACM abstract; authors via ACM PDF/ResearchGate). 80-article review: user autonomy is not independent task execution but the *sustained capacity to author one's goals* while the system co-participates; consolidates five categories of autonomy-support design mechanisms.
  - 2025, Astobiza AM. "Do AI agents trump human agency?" *Discover Artificial Intelligence* 5:348. DOI 10.1007/s44163-025-00608-y. VERIFIED (open access). "Obsolescence regime" thesis: efficiency-optimized agents can marginalize human evaluator discretion (hiring case study: biased LLM recommendations limit agency); asks that AI enhance rather than undermine human agency.
  - 2026, Xu K, Shen Y, Yan L, Ren Y. "Cognitive Agency Surrender: Defending Epistemic Sovereignty via Scaffolded AI Friction." arXiv:2603.21735. VERIFIED. Bibliometric analysis of 1,223 AI-HCI papers (2023-2026): research defending epistemic sovereignty declined (19.1% → 13.1%) while autonomous-agent optimization surged (19.6%); proposes deliberate "scaffolded cognitive friction" (e.g., computational devil's advocate) as an agency-preserving mechanism.
  - 2025, Yu R, Wan S, Wang Y, Gao CX, Gan L, Zhang Z, Zhan DC. "Reward Models in Deep Reinforcement Learning: A Survey." IJCAI 2025 Survey Track. arXiv:2506.15421. VERIFIED. Supplies the technical sense of empowerment: information-theoretic intrinsic motivation (Klyubin et al.) maximizing an agent's influence over future outcomes; empowerment-based skill learning yields generalizable behavior — a formalizable objective relevant to assistive agents.
- **Key papers 2024 (pre-window, foundational):**
  - 2024, Yu F, Moehring A, Banerjee O, et al. "Heterogeneity and predictors of the effects of AI assistance on radiologists." *Nature Medicine* 30:837-849. DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-02850-w. VERIFIED. 140 radiologists, 15 tasks: experience/subspecialty/AI familiarity do not predict assistance benefit; lower performers do not consistently benefit more; AI error incidence dominates outcomes — the canonical case for *personalized* assistive strategies.
- **Convergent findings:** "Assistance ≠ empowerment" — help that lifts task performance can erode independent capability (Brynjolfsson vs. Area 1); perceived agency is a distinct, powerful driver of engagement that is decoupled from actual capacity gains (Beacock); autonomy support must be explicitly designed via identifiable mechanisms (Wang taxonomy), not assumed from helpfulness; heterogeneity is the rule, so assistance must adapt to the individual (Yu); "friction" can be protective, not just a UX cost (Xu, Bastani).
- **Contrary/conflicting findings:** Brynjolfsson's productivity gains vs. the deskilling literature — resolvable only by measuring capability separately from performance, which almost no study does; Beacock's agency-based adoption model directly contradicts trust-based models of AI use; empowerment rhetoric risks "empowerment theater" — perceived gains without material gains (Beacock's own tension finding); Astobiza's agency-erosion argument vs. the "augmentation" camp — unresolved normative split on whether assistance replaces or enhances discretion.
- **Gaps:** No validated, objective measures distinguishing empowered from dependent use; no longitudinal RCTs with user-capability (not task-performance) outcomes; technical RL "empowerment" is agent-centric — formalizing *user* empowerment (the user's influence over outcomes) as an optimization target is open; group/multi-user empowerment unstudied; no design pattern catalog linking the Wang autonomy mechanisms to measurable agency outcomes.
- **Agent-architecture implications:** Set the agent's objective as maximizing the user's long-run capability (empowerment), not task completion; detect "agency illusion" — perceived-gain-without-gain patterns (Beacock) — and correct for it; implement autonomy-support mechanisms (control surfaces, transparency, user modeling, adaptive autonomy levels) per the Wang taxonomy; treat friction as a first-class design parameter; consider formalizing user-influence (mutual-information-style) objectives adapted from RL empowerment; personalize assistance allocation per user (Yu) rather than one-size-fits-all.
- **Source fidelity flags:** VERIFIED — Brynjolfsson 2025, Beacock 2026, Wang 2026 (CHI EA), Astobiza 2025, Xu 2026, Yu 2025 (IJCAI survey), Yu 2024 (Nat Med). RECONSTRUCTED — mapping of Wang's five mechanism categories and of RL-empowerment to home-lab agent objectives (interpretation, labeled as such). No UNVERIFIED items used as evidence.

## Area: Proactive/personalized agents

- **Key papers 2025-2026:**
  - 2026, Li G, Pan L, Wang L. "ProEvent: An Event-centric Benchmark for Proactive Agents." arXiv:2607.17701. VERIFIED. First event-centric benchmark (timetable maintenance from chat streams): even GPT-5.1 reacts correctly in only 26.7% of scenarios; agents overact and miss event cancellations; implicit-event detection and first-person user-perspective reasoning are the core failure modes.
  - 2026, Chen Z, Duan C, Sun K, et al. "UniClawBench: A Universal Benchmark for Proactive Agents on Real-World Tasks." arXiv:2607.08768. VERIFIED. 400 bilingual real-world tasks evaluated in live Docker containers with closed-loop executor/supervisor/user agents; decomposes proactivity into five capabilities (skill usage, exploration, long-context reasoning, multimodal understanding, cross-platform coordination); shows base-model and framework choices jointly shape performance.
  - 2026, Ma X, Qiu J, Yao Y, et al. "Communication Policy Evolution for Proactive LLM Agents." arXiv:2606.14314. VERIFIED. Formalizes communication policy under information asymmetry; text and UI channels have complementary strengths; CPE self-evolution via prompt-only refinement achieves best task success — communication behavior is an underexplored design dimension.
  - 2026, Yang S, Xu D, Pei J, Wang D. "ProACT: Towards Breakdown-Aware Proactive Agent in Multi-User Collaboration." arXiv:2607.03730. VERIFIED. Grounded in common-ground/coordination theory; explicitly decides *whether to stay silent or speak* at each turn; improves appropriateness, non-interruptiveness, and conciseness across 3,244 turns and five LLM backbones.
  - 2025, Pasternak G, Rajagopal D, White J, Atreja D, Thomas M, Hurn-Maloney G, Lewis A. "Beyond Reactivity: Measuring Proactive Problem Solving in LLM Agents" (PROBE). arXiv:2510.19771. VERIFIED. Decomposes proactivity into search-for-unspecified-issues / identify-bottlenecks / execute-resolutions; best end-to-end score is 40% (GPT-5, Claude Opus 4.1) — frontier models remain far from autonomous proactivity.
  - 2025, Yang B, Xu L, Zeng L, et al. "ProAgent: Harnessing On-Demand Sensory Contexts for Proactive LLM Agent Systems in the Wild." arXiv:2512.06721. VERIFIED. AR-glasses system with on-demand tiered sensing and preference integration: +27.7% proactive-prediction accuracy, −20.5% false detections, 85% user satisfaction (n=20) — proactivity as continuous ambient sensing, not schedules.
  - 2026, Liu A, Karoui Y, Draxler F, Kreuter F, Chiossi F. "Sensing What Surveys Miss: Understanding and Personalizing Proactive LLM Support by User Modeling." arXiv:2602.00880 (CHI 2026). VERIFIED. Physiological (EDA) + behavioral (mouse) user-state classifiers with adaptive thresholding: aligned-adaptive intervention timing improved accuracy +21% and cut false negatives from 50.9% to 22.9% vs. misaligned/random timing — timing is a personalization axis, not a constant.
  - 2025, Chen V, Zhu A, Zhao S, Mozannar H, Sontag D, Talwalkar A. "Need Help? Designing Proactive AI Assistants for Programming." *CHI '25*. DOI 10.1145/3706598.3714002 / arXiv:2410.04596. VERIFIED. User-centered design study of proactive assistance: users want control over proactivity, valued intervention types differ by expertise, and timing preferences are heterogeneous.
  - 2026, Xu Y, Chen Q, Ma Z, et al. "Toward Personalized LLM-Powered Agents: Foundations, Evaluation, and Future Directions." arXiv:2602.22680. VERIFIED. Survey organizing personalized agents as four interdependent capabilities — profile modeling, memory, planning, action execution — with evaluation gaps flagged per capability.
- **Convergent findings:** Proactivity is currently the bottleneck capability of LLM agents — benchmarks converge on a 26-40% ceiling regardless of model; false-positive over-action is as prevalent as under-action; intervention *timing* and *restraint* matter as much as content (ProACT silence decisions, Liu timing study); evaluation must be closed-loop, multi-turn, and real-world (UniClawBench, ProEvent) because static single-turn tests overstate capability; personalization and proactivity are coupled — user modeling is required to decide when (not just what) to act; modality choice is a real design lever (CPE).
- **Contrary/conflicting findings:** Channel effects are non-unidirectional (text boosts task performance while UI boosts persona compliance — CPE); a tension exists between proactivity metrics that reward initiation and non-interruptiveness/social cost (ProACT explicitly trades these off); UniClawBench finds model and framework jointly matter, while PROBE attributes most variance to model capability — attribution of proactive failure is contested; no consensus on whether agents should ask-before-acting or act-then-notify (Chen vs. ProAgent).
- **Gaps:** No standard metric weighting interruption cost against intervention benefit; long-horizon personalization (months of memory, drifting preferences) is unevaluated; multi-user/group proactivity is nascent (only ProACT); privacy of continuous sensing is unresolved; no work integrates skill-preservation or empowerment constraints into proactive behavior (proactive agents that train rather than replace); "when NOT to act" is not yet a benchmarked capability.
- **Agent-architecture implications:** Implement an explicit intervention policy over (user state, expected benefit, interruption cost) — grounded in observable user state (sensing/behavior) rather than fixed schedules; penalize over-action as a first-class failure mode and expose silence decisions (ProACT); build profile-modeling + memory as first-class components (Xu taxonomy) with timing personalization (Liu); use mixed-initiative consent (propose-then-act) for high-cost interventions; evaluate on closed-loop, multi-turn, real-world tasks; treat modality (chat vs. UI) as a tunable policy dimension; pair proactivity with the Area-1 constraint — interventions should scaffold, not replace, user skill.
- **Source fidelity flags:** VERIFIED — ProEvent, UniClawBench, CPE, ProACT, PROBE, ProAgent, Liu 2026 (CHI), Chen 2025 (CHI), Xu 2026 (survey). RECONSTRUCTED — synthesis that proactive success hinges on timing/restraint and that proactivity evaluation needs cost-weighting (interpretation across papers). No UNVERIFIED items used as evidence.

**Synthesis (3-5 sentences):** Across the three literatures, the post-2024 evidence converges on one governing tension for the Motivational Ecology Agent Architecture: assistance that maximizes immediate task performance reliably erodes independent capability (Bastani, Budzyń, Natali) unless it is deliberately scaffolded, while engagement itself is driven by perceived agency gains that can be illusory (Beacock) — so empowerment must be an engineered objective, not an assumed side effect. Proactivity adds the trigger dimension: the benchmark literature shows it is currently the ceiling capability of agents (26-40% success), and its value hinges entirely on timing, restraint, and personalization (ProACT, Liu, CPE). The architecture should therefore treat user long-run capability as the primary objective, impose minimal-sufficiency assistance (hints, not answers) as a skill-preservation constraint, and implement proactivity as a policy over (user state, interruption cost, expected benefit) with explicit silence options. The most productive open design space — one all three literatures flag as empty — is an agent that measures its own effect on user skill and adapts its assistance and proactivity accordingly; such an agent would simultaneously address the deskilling evidence, the empowerment-formalization gap, and the missing "skill-aware" proactive benchmark.
