## Framework: Self-Determination Theory (SDT)

- **Canonical sources**: Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2000). Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being. *American Psychologist, 55*(1), 68–78. **VERIFIED** (full PDF fetched). Ryan & Deci (2017). *Self-Determination Theory: Basic Psychological Needs in Motivation, Development, and Wellness*. Guilford. **UNVERIFIED** (not fetched; existence confirmed only via Vansteenkiste et al. 2020 citation). Deci & Ryan (1985), *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior*, Plenum. **UNVERIFIED** (cited inside Ryan & Deci 2000).
- **Core constructs**: (1) Intrinsic motivation — the inherent propensity toward novelty, mastery, spontaneous interest, exploration (Ryan & Deci 2000). (2) Extrinsic motivation continuum — amotivation → external → introjected → identified → integrated → intrinsic, ordered by "relative autonomy" (quasi-simplex, Ryan & Connell 1989; VERIFIED). (3) Three basic psychological needs — competence, autonomy, relatedness — "innate, essential, universal" nutriments; satisfaction → well-being, thwarting → ill-being (VERIFIED). (4) Autonomy, in SDT, means volition, *not* independence/individualism (explicitly defended, VERIFIED). (5) Causality orientations — **RECONSTRUCTED** (trait-level autonomy/control/impersonal orientations; from the 2017 book, not fetched; standard SDT content). (6) Autonomy support — social-contextual conditions that facilitate versus forestall self-motivation (VERIFIED).
- **Claimed mechanism**: Social contexts catalyze or suppress intrinsic motivation (CET: competence + autonomy needed; positive feedback enhances, expected tangible rewards undermine). Internalization (OIT): relatedness is central for uptake, competence for adoption, and autonomy support — via "deep, holistic processing" — enables integration of regulations into the self; contexts supporting all three needs yield autonomous regulation, which yields persistence, creativity, vitality, and eudaimonia (VERIFIED; Deci, Eghrari, Patrick & Leone 1994 experiment: rationale + autonomy/relatedness support promoted internalization).
- **Signature methods/instruments**: Experimental manipulation of context (Baconian tradition, VERIFIED); Ryan & Connell (1989) Self-Regulation Questionnaire approach / relative-autonomy composites (VERIFIED); Basic Psychological Need Satisfaction and Frustration Scale (BPNSFS), with numerous translations (VERIFIED via Vansteenkiste et al. 2020, Table 3); autonomy-support climate scales (e.g., HCCQ/LCQ) and Intrinsic Motivation Inventory — **RECONSTRUCTED** (well-established but not fetched in this pass).
- **Evidence landmarks**: Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999) meta-analysis — all expected tangible rewards contingent on performance reliably undermine intrinsic motivation (VERIFIED; explicitly contra Eisenberger & Cameron 1996, flagged as the contrary claim). Kasser & Ryan (1993, 1996) — intrinsic aspirations predict well-being, extrinsic (wealth/fame/image) do not; replicated in Russia (VERIFIED). Sheldon, Reis & Ryan (1996) / Reis et al. — within-person daily need satisfaction predicts daily mood, vitality, symptoms (VERIFIED). Ntoumanis et al. (2021), *Health Psychology Review* 15(2):214–244, meta of 73 SDT-informed interventions — modest positive effects on health behavior; increases in need support and autonomous motivation (not controlled motivation/amotivation) associated with behavior change (**VERIFIED**); Gillison et al. (2019) — autonomy support g=0.84, autonomy satisfaction g=0.81, competence g=0.63, relatedness g=0.28 (VERIFIED via Ntoumanis 2021's review of it).
- **Tensions/open questions**: Need *satisfaction* vs need *frustration* as distinct (not opposite-pole) processes predicting ill-being/vulnerability (VERIFIED, Vansteenkiste, Ryan & Soenens 2020); universality vs cultural variability of need effects (VERIFIED as debated); proposals to extend the needs list beyond three (VERIFIED as discussed); measurement critiques (self-report scales; "needs" definitional criteria); effect sizes modest and heterogeneous in health domains (Ntoumanis 2021).
- **Agent-operability notes**: An LLM agent can operationalize autonomy-supportive phrasing (non-controlling language, meaningful rationale, perspective-taking, choice menus), run need-support checks on its own utterances (BPNSFS-style probes), classify user speech into regulatory styles (external/introjected/identified/intrinsic) to target integration rather than compliance, and avoid performance-contingent tangible reward mechanics that predictably undermine intrinsic motivation. Gerstenberg et al. (2025), *Frontiers in Psychology* — SDT applied to behavior-change technologies (**VERIFIED**, page fetched): feasibility of need-based design audit for digital tools.
- **Home-lab relevance**: Personal-development agents should default to autonomy-first defaults (explicit "you decide" affordances), scaffold competence with optimal challenge and effectance feedback, foster relatedness, and treat introjection signals (guilt/anxiety-driven language) as alarms, not wins.

Causality orientations, IMI, climate scales — RECONSTRUCTED/UNVERIFIED (not fetched). Ryan & Deci 2017 book — UNVERIFIED.

## Framework: Motivational Interviewing (MI)

- **Canonical sources**: Miller, W. R., & Rose, G. S. (2009). Toward a theory of motivational interviewing. *American Psychologist, 64*(6), 527–537. **VERIFIED** (full PMC text). Miller & Rollnick (2013), *Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change*, 3rd ed., Guilford — **UNVERIFIED directly**, but its four-process/spirit/OARS content is **VERIFIED via Bischof et al. 2021**, which quotes and boxes it. Miller, W. R. (2023). The evolution of motivational interviewing. *Behavioural and Cognitive Psychotherapy*. **VERIFIED** (full PDF). Miller (1983), *Behavioural Psychotherapy* — **UNVERIFIED** (cited within Miller & Rose 2009).
- **Core constructs**: (1) Spirit — partnership ("communication on equal terms," clinician not expert), acceptance/empathy, compassion (patient's needs prioritized), and evocation (exploring/reinforcing the patient's own reasons; VERIFIED, Bischof 2021 Box 1); Rollnick & Miller (1995) earlier triad: collaborative, evocative, honors client autonomy (VERIFIED via Miller & Rose). (2) Four processes — **Engaging** (relationship building), **Focusing** (agreeing direction/priorities), **Evoking** (eliciting the client's own change talk), **Planning** (commitment → concrete action); non-linear, revisitable (VERIFIED, Bischof 2021 Box 2). (3) OARS skills — Open questions (≥70% of questions), Affirmations, Reflections (≥50% complex; ≥2 reflections per question), Summaries (VERIFIED fidelity heuristics via Bischof). (4) Change talk vs sustain talk; DARN-CAT (Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need → Commitment, Activation, Taking steps; VERIFIED, Bischof). (5) Miller–Rose theory: a *relational* component (empathy, MI spirit) and a *technical* component (differential evocation and reinforcement of change talk) (VERIFIED).
- **Claimed mechanism**: A causal chain — MI training → therapist behavior → client in-session speech (more change talk, less sustain talk) → strengthening commitment language → behavior change; relational pathway runs empathy/spirit → client involvement/outcome independently (VERIFIED, Miller & Rose Figure 1). Theoretical roots: self-perception (Bem), cognitive dissonance (Festinger), Rogers' necessary conditions (VERIFIED).
- **Signature methods/instruments**: Motivational Interviewing Skill Code (MISC) and MI Treatment Integrity (MITI) coding of practice samples — self-reported proficiency is *unrelated* to coded proficiency (Miller & Mount 2001, VERIFIED); elicit–provide–elicit for information-giving (VERIFIED); FRAMES brief-intervention components (VERIFIED); the Drinker's Check-Up / MET format (VERIFIED).
- **Evidence landmarks**: Therapist empathy predicted ~two-thirds of outcome variance in drinking (r=.82 at 6 months; r=.71 at 12 mo; r=.51 at 24 mo — Miller et al. 1980; Miller & Baca 1983, VERIFIED). MI-style feedback doubled change talk and halved resistance vs confrontational style in the same counselors (Miller et al. 1993, VERIFIED). A single MI session doubled abstinence when added to treatment (Brown & Miller 1993; Bien et al. 1993; Aubrey 1998, VERIFIED). Amrhein et al. (2003) — *strength* and *slope* of commitment language (not frequency/intercept of change talk) predicted outcomes; DARN are preparatory, commitment is prognostic (VERIFIED). Moyers et al. (2007) — MI-consistent therapist utterances → change talk; MI-inconsistent → sustain talk; both independently predicted drinking (VERIFIED). Bischof et al. (2021) meta-analytic OR 1.55 [1.40–1.71] across medical-care behaviors; effects "largely through selective reinforcement of patient change statements" (**VERIFIED**). **Contrary**: null trials (eating disorders, smoking, some drug trials; site-by-site variability in multisite trials — VERIFIED, Miller & Rose "Mixed Findings"); Hettema et al. (2005) effect doubled in minority populations (VERIFIED); workshop-only training fails to change practice (VERIFIED); MI delivered without spirit shows no effect (Kuchipudi et al. 1990, VERIFIED); smaller/less consistent effects on psychiatric symptoms — **UNVERIFIED** (search snippet only, Bahri 2025).
- **Tensions/open questions**: Relative contributions of relational vs technical components unresolved (Sellman et al. 2001 supports technical; Karno & Longabaugh 2005 reactance interaction — VERIFIED); frequency-vs-strength measurement issue; disingenuous change talk; fidelity requires coded observation; effect-size debates outside substance use; MI usually combined with other treatments (Miller 2023 — its most common use).
- **Agent-operability notes**: An LLM agent can implement evocation (ask, don't prescribe), reflective listening (complex reflections), the elicit–provide–elicit protocol, autonomy emphasis at signs of discord ("Only you can decide..."), and DARN-CAT detection/strengthening with commitment-slope tracking over time. Guardrail: MI *without* the spirit is not MI — technical manipulation without partnership/acceptance fails (Kuchipudi; Miller & Rose discussion). Computer-based adaptations of the check-up have proven effective (Hester et al. 2005; Walters et al. 2007b, VERIFIED via Miller 2023) — precedent for conversational agents.
- **Home-lab relevance**: The interaction layer of a personal-development agent: navigate ambivalence, surface the user's own reasons, convert motivation to commitment and plans, without ever arguing against the user's resistance.
- **Source fidelity flags**: Spirit, four processes, OARS, DARN-CAT, efficacy OR — VERIFIED (Bischof 2021 full text; Miller & Rose 2009 full text; Miller 2023 full text). Miller & Rollnick 2013 book — UNVERIFIED directly (content verified via Bischof's boxed exposition). Bahri 2025 psychiatric-effects critique — UNVERIFIED (search snippet).

## Framework: Practice Theory

- **Canonical sources**: Reckwitz, A. (2002). Toward a theory of social practices: A development in culturalist theorizing. *European Journal of Social Theory, 5*(2), 243–263. **VERIFIED** (full PDF). Shove, E., Pantzar, M., & Watson, M. (2012). *The Dynamics of Social Practice: Everyday Life and How It Changes*. Sage. **VERIFIED via detailed secondary exposition** (forumviesmobiles.org book summary with direct quotes/pp. 82, 96; SAGE TOC listing) — book itself not directly fetched. Schatzki (1996), *Social Practices: A Wittgensteinian Approach*, CUP — **UNVERIFIED** (characterized via Reckwitz and Welch). Schatzki (2002), *The Site of the Social* — **UNVERIFIED directly** (content verified via Welch's footnotes).
- **Core constructs**: (1) A practice is "a routinized way in which bodies are moved, objects are handled, subjects are treated, things are described and the world is understood" — a nexus of doings and sayings (Schatzki) composed of bodily *and* mental routines (VERIFIED, Reckwitz). (2) Practice-as-entity (recognizable pattern/ideal script) vs practice-as-performance (each enactment is a scripted-yet-open repetition carrying novelty) (VERIFIED, Shove et al. via exposition). (3) Three elements — materials (things, technologies), competences (skills, know-how, understanding), meanings (symbolic significance, aspirations); practices exist when elements are actively integrated; change = breaking/recombining links; practices form bundles/complexes and share elements ("connective tissue" across practices) (VERIFIED via exposition, pp. 82, 96). (4) Routinization is the site of social structure; the individual is the unique "crossing point" of many practices, a carrier (Träger) (VERIFIED, Reckwitz). (5) Things are constitutive of the social, not mere instruments (VERIFIED, Reckwitz).
- **Claimed mechanism**: Social order is reproduced through routine bodily-mental performance rather than norms, interests, or discourse alone; practices recruit carriers and persist via repetition; change arises from unfaithful repetitions, crises of routine, and re-linkage of elements (VERIFIED, Reckwitz §Structure/Process; Shove et al. via exposition).
- **Signature methods/instruments**: No standardized scales — ethnographic observation, practice mapping, element-linkage analysis, analysis of bundles/complexes and rhythms (frequency, duration, sequence) (VERIFIED via exposition; Reckwitz frames the theory as a heuristic "vocabulary," not a truth-claiming system, §4).
- **Evidence landmarks**: Reckwitz (2002) — canonical systematization of the practice-theory family (VERIFIED). Shove et al. (2012) driving example — element change (reliable gasoline engine) reconfigured competences and meanings, transforming the practice itself (VERIFIED via exposition). Klitkou et al. (2022), *Sustainable Production and Consumption* 31:603–614 — practice fields (working, dwelling, mobility, eating, recreation) co-evolve and co-locate; sustainability requires interventions across interacting practice fields, not technology fixes or individual behavior change alone (**VERIFIED**, abstract). Welch (2017), *Social Business* 7(3–4):241–261 — practice perspectives offer more robust accounts of change and reframe the attitude-behavior/value-action gap, but efficacy "has yet to be empirically established" (**VERIFIED**, full PDF).
- **Tensions/open questions**: Heterogeneity of definitions across lineages (Welch 2017 note i, VERIFIED); Shove's three-element model vs Schatzki's practices + material-arrangements distinction (Welch note iii, VERIFIED); practice theory's "portfolio model" critique of individualistic behavior change (Welch, VERIFIED) vs counter-critique of individualistic implications in some Schatzki readings (Welch & Warde 2017, RECONSTRUCTED via footnote); neglect of work-practice interconnectedness (Klitkou 2022, VERIFIED); no established intervention evidence base (Welch 2017, VERIFIED).
- **Agent-operability notes**: Represent the user's life as a graph of practices × elements (materials/competences/meanings) with typed links — directly mappable to skill nodes and edges. Intervene on elements and links (change the material environment, teach a competence, reframe a meaning) rather than attitudes alone; model routines with frequency/duration/sequence; detect shared elements bridging practices (one competence serving two practices); design for "unfaithful repetition" so novelty enters routine.
- **Home-lab relevance**: Personal-development agents that only boost motivation (SDT/MI) neglect that behavior lives inside practices; durable change requires re-engineering the practice ecology — cues, tools, skills, and meanings together — and respecting that practices recruit the user, not vice versa.
- **Source fidelity flags**: Reckwitz 2002 core constructs — VERIFIED (full text). Shove et al. 2012 — VERIFIED via faithful secondary exposition with page-level quotes; book UNVERIFIED directly. Schatzki 1996/2002 — UNVERIFIED (secondary characterizations only). Post-2020 application — VERIFIED (Klitkou 2022 abstract). Critique — VERIFIED (Welch 2017 full PDF). Welch & Warde 2017 — RECONSTRUCTED.

**Synthesis**: SDT supplies the motivational core — why people internalize and persist (need satisfaction, autonomy-first) — MI supplies the interaction protocol — how an agent can evoke and strengthen the user's own reasons while honoring autonomy, never manipulating (relational + technical components, four processes) — and practice theory supplies the ecological frame: motivation is enacted inside routines of materials, competences, and meanings that recruit the user. For the agent architecture, the highest-value transferable elements are: (1) SDT-derived design constraints and need-support checks on agent utterances, (2) MI's evocation techniques, DARN-CAT/commitment tracking, and spirit guardrails as the conversational layer, and (3) practice theory's element-linkage graphs and routine-level intervention targets as the world-model — together yielding skill nodes with typed edges for need-support relations, process transitions (engaging→focusing→evoking→planning), and practice-element links, with autonomy preservation as an architectural invariant rather than a style choice.
