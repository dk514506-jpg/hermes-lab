## 3. Fault lines (must not be smoothed over)

1. **Verbeek's moral mediation vs. Winner's inscribed politics:** Verbeek says technologies *mediate* moral action (shape the landscape of decision). Winner says artifacts *inscribe* politics (settle issues by configuration). These are complementary but not identical: mediation is about the *process* of moral experience; inscription is about the *structural* outcome. A tool that mediates morally (Verbeek) may still inscribe bad politics (Winner). *Resolution:* apply both — mediation asks "what does the tool do to moral experience?"; inscription asks "what politics does the tool settle?"

2. **Borgmann's focal practices vs. Simondon's individuation:** Borgmann says focal practices (engagement, contestability, ultimate concern) center and order life. Simondon says individuation is a process of becoming-through-relations. These pull in different directions: Borgmann's focal practices are *stable* (the fireplace, the family meal); Simondon's individuation is *processual* (always metastable, always becoming). *Resolution:* focal practices are the *stabilizations* that individuation temporarily produces; individuation is the *process* that focal practices crystallize. Both matter.

3. **Ellul's autonomous technique vs. Feenberg's democratic rationalization:** Ellul says technique is autonomous and self-augmenting — it escapes human direction. Feenberg says technical rationality contains an indeterminist margin where democratic participation is possible. These are in direct tension: if technique is fully autonomous (Ellul), democratic rationalization (Feenberg) is impossible; if democratic rationalization is possible (Feenberg), technique is not fully autonomous (Ellul). *Resolution:* this is the central live tension of the A1 spine. Hold it. Ellul is the *warning*; Feenberg is the *possibility*. The truth is likely somewhere between — technique has autonomous tendencies that can be resisted but never fully overcome.

4. **Hui's cosmotechnic pluralism vs. Ihde's universal grammar:** Hui says there are *multiple* cosmotechnics — different unifications of cosmos, morality, and technics. Ihde offers a *universal* grammar of human-technology relations (embodiment, hermeneutic, alterity, background) that applies across cultures. *Resolution:* Ihde's grammar is a *formal* taxonomy of relation-types; Hui's cosmotechnics is a *substantive* claim about the content of cosmos-morality-technics unifications. The same formal relation-type (e.g., embodiment) can mediate different cosmotechnic contents.

5. **A1-10 Verbeek partial-text caveat:** Only 2 core chapters (ch.5-6, ch.7-8) are charted. The full book's arguments (the opening framing, the phenomenology of things, the closing synthesis) are NOT in front of me. This is a chapter-coverage charting with documented caveat — flagged as verification debt for the full institutional copy.

6. **A1-19 Verbeek abstract-level caveat:** The row is charted at the abstract/thesis-statement level only. No schedule/mechanism/quote claims beyond the abstract. This is the ONLY remaining no-full-text row in the A1 spine — flagged as verification debt gated on user-supplied full text.

## 4. Tier-C pilot implications (binding)

- **Democratic rationalization (A1-08 Feenberg):** The design space is politically open — there is more than one technically viable way to build a tool. The person affected by the tool has a legitimate claim to participate in its design. The artifact must be revisable by its users (S10 reversibility).

- **Four relation-types (A1-09 Ihde):** Ask which relation-type the tool establishes with the user. Embodiment and hermeneutic relations are compatible with user authorship; alterity and background relations risk black-boxing. The tool should NOT disappear into pure background.

- **Moral mediation (A1-10 Verbeek):** The artifact is a moral mediator — it shapes what the user perceives and does, and thereby shapes the moral landscape of decision. Design is experimental ethics — ethics accompanies the design process from the inside.

- **Daoist/Tragist typology (A1-03 Hui):** The tool can be oriented toward letting-be (Daoist) or heroic action (Tragist). The artifact should resist the recursive closure of the user's sensibility into a feedback loop.

- **Device paradigm (A1-13 Borgmann):** The tool must be evaluated against the device paradigm: does it make a commodity available without the practice that gives it meaning? If so, it is a device in Borgmann's sense — veto territory. Protect practice from convenience extraction.

- **Technical autonomy warning (A1-17 Ellul):** Warning about technical autonomy — the tool must not become self-augmenting in ways that escape human direction. The Design Veto Catalogue must ask: does this tool establish a logic that, once adopted, is difficult to reverse?

- **Individuation (A1-06 Simondon):** The person is an individuating system — the tool must NOT treat the user as a fixed, pre-constituted agent. It should support the user's ongoing individuation — their becoming-through-practice — without rigid identity protocols.
