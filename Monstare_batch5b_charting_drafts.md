## A2-01 — Li, Dey & Forlizzi, Stage-Based Model of Personal Informatics (CHI 2010)
**Source read:** Author PDF (full paper). Text layer verified. Complete.
**Pri A | CR high | Function: MECHANISM/DESIGN/METHOD | Evidence Type: theoretical | Causal Status: conceptual**

**Key Finding / Thesis:**
Personal informatics systems help people collect and reflect on personal information. The paper derives a stage-based model: five stages (preparation, collection, integration, reflection, action) with barriers in each. Key properties: barriers cascade to later stages; stages are iterative; they are user-driven and/or system-driven; they are uni-faceted or multi-faceted. Design recommendations: design holistically across stages; allow iteration; balance automation and user control; support associating multiple facets of people's lives. Without reflection, collection becomes data hoarding without insight.

**Effect Size / Strength:** not_quantifiable (theoretical). Strength = high as the MECHANISM/DESIGN/METHOD row — the five-stage model is the explicit warrant for the Digital Capture Architecture (construct-role claim).

**Limitations:** CHI paper (8pp). Survey/interview based (N unknown from extract). The model is prescriptive, not empirically validated. The barriers are identified but not quantified.

**Disconfirming Implication:** If users do NOT progress through stages iteratively (if they skip reflection and go straight to action), the model's predictions fail.

**H1:** partial | **H2:** yes
**Design Implication:** Design capture workflow; prevent data hoarding without reflection. The Digital Capture Architecture must support all five stages, with reflection as the critical integration point.
**Cosmotechnic Implication:** Capture must serve reflection, not accumulation. The tool's purpose is self-knowledge, not data collection.
**Causal Status:** conceptual

## A2-02 — Lupton, The Quantified Self (Polity 2018, ch.1)
**Source read:** Chapter PDF (full ch.1). Text layer verified. Complete.
**Pri A | CR high | Function: CRITIQUE/WARNING | Evidence Type: critique | Causal Status: conceptual**

**Key Finding / Thesis:**
Self-tracking datafies selfhood; risks neoliberal self-management. The quantified self is not new (Franklin tracked virtues for 60 years) but digital technologies have transformed the practice. Self-tracking can represent taking control OR signify weakness/lack of self-discipline. For some, it is empowering; for others, it produces anxiety, social pressure, and a sense of being constantly monitored. The sociological critique: self-tracking embeds neoliberal ideals of optimization, productivity, and individual responsibility for health/well-being.

**Effect Size / Strength:** not_quantifiable (critique). Strength = high as the CRITIQUE/WARNING row — the datafication-of-selfhood thesis is the warrant for the Anti-Extraction Audit (construct-role claim).

**Limitations:** Sociological critique, not empirical study. Chapter 1 of a book — the full argument develops across the volume. The critique is broad, not specific to particular tools or designs.

**Disconfirming Implication:** If self-tracking does NOT datafy selfhood (if users experience it as playful, social, or identity-affirming), the critique overstates the neoliberal capture.

**H1:** no | **H2:** yes
**Design Implication:** Name metric-capture and self-discipline risks. The tool must not reduce the user to a set of metrics or embed neoliberal optimization imperatives.
**Cosmotechnic Implication:** Numbers can displace self-knowledge. The tool must preserve the user's lived, embodied self-relation against datafication.
**Causal Status:** conceptual

## A3-02 — Bennett, Metatla, Roudaut & Mekler, How does HCI Understand Human Agency and Autonomy? (CHI 2023)
**Source read:** arXiv PDF (full paper, 18pp). Text layer verified. Complete.
**Pri A | CR high | Function: REVIEW/CRITIQUE/DESIGN | Evidence Type: review | Causal Status: conceptual**

**Key Finding / Thesis:**
A systematic literature review (161 papers, 1990-2022) finds that "autonomy" and "agency" in HCI are vague umbrella constructs — used interchangeably, given a wide range of meanings (implicit experience of self-causality, material influence, experience of congruence with values). Few works give specific definitions. The paper identifies four aspects that distinguish approaches: (1) self-causality and personal identity; (2) experience vs. material expression; (3) time-scales; (4) independence vs. interdependence. It calls for HCI to develop these as well-functioning boundary objects — flexible enough to coordinate across communities, robust enough to maintain identity.

**Effect Size / Strength:** not_quantifiable (review). Strength = high as the REVIEW/CRITIQUE/DESIGN row — the umbrella-construct critique is the warrant for the Agency & Autonomy Checklist's explicit definitions (construct-role claim).

**Limitations:** Review paper — the analysis is descriptive and taxonomic, not prescriptive. The 161-paper corpus is comprehensive but the findings are conceptual, not empirical.

**Disconfirming Implication:** If autonomy and agency ARE already well-defined in HCI (if the review's critique is overstated), the call for boundary objects is unnecessary.

**H1:** no | **H2:** yes
**Design Implication:** Force explicit agency criteria; kill vague autonomy talk. The Agency & Autonomy Checklist must require explicit definitions of what "agency" and "autonomy" mean for each tool/artifact.
**Cosmotechnic Implication:** Autonomy must be operationalized. The cosmotechnic audit cannot rely on vague appeals to "autonomy" — it must specify what kind, for whom, in what context.
**Causal Status:** conceptual
