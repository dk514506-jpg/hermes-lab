## CORE-03 — Vansteenkiste, Williams & Resnicow 2012 (SDT/MI integration)
- **Key Finding / Thesis**: SDT (top-down, theory-driven) and MI (bottom-up, practice-driven) are complementary developmental pathways; systematic integration is possible and desirable. The integration hinge: MI researchers should embrace autonomy/volition as the fundamental basic process underlying therapeutic change; autonomy-supportive communication is the practical grammar connecting the two.
- **Effect Size / Strength**: not_quantifiable — conceptual/theoretical integration paper (argues, does not measure).
- **Limitations**: The integration is a proposal, not a tested intervention; the paper argues from the two literatures; SDT's universality claims vs MI's pragmatic roots remain a live tension (batch-1 fault line 5); no new data.
- **Disconfirming Implication**: If MI's efficacy were shown to operate through mechanisms other than autonomy/volition (e.g., alliance, expectancy), the integration hinge weakens; if SDT's autonomy construct does not map cleanly onto MI's spirit, the proposed synthesis is decorative.
- **H1**: yes — autonomy support improves sustained motivation and behavior change.
- **H2**: partial — volition as a vector (quality of motivation), not compliance-as-output.
- **Design Implication**: Unify the tool's motivational grammar around volition (autonomy support: choice, rationale, acknowledgment of resistance); M2 need-support profile stays a design heuristic, not a validated manipulation (batch-1 pilot rule). Telos remains the person's own (CORE-02 ruling carries).
- **Cosmotechnic Implication**: Volition over compliance — the tool must never purchase behavior through compliance-shaped grammar; autonomy support is the criterion, and coercion-by-nudge is a false integration (Tier-P application under A1-01/HUI-2024 criterion).
- **Causal Status (draft)**: conceptual. **Evidence Type (draft)**: theoretical (integration proposal; not a review per se).
- **Cosmo Rel.**: medium. **Notes for QC**: check evidence-type taxonomy (theoretical vs review).

## CORE-11 — Ordonez et al. 2009 (Goals Gone Wild)
- **Key Finding / Thesis**: The beneficial effects of goal setting are overstated; specific, challenging goals produce systematic side effects: narrow focus that neglects non-goal areas, rise in unethical behavior, distorted risk preferences, corrosion of organizational culture, reduced intrinsic motivation. Goal setting should be treated as prescription-strength medication: careful dosing, side-effect monitoring, close supervision, warning label (10-question caution list, incl. goal type performance vs learning, who sets goals, time horizon, risk articulation, intrinsic-motivation check).
- **Effect Size / Strength**: not_quantifiable — narrative/argumentative review with case evidence (Sears, Enron-era cases); no pooled ES; the replicated positive linear difficulty–performance relation (Locke & Latham) is acknowledged but not re-quantified.
- **Limitations**: Argumentative review; selective emblematic cases; authors are critics of the dominant paradigm (advocacy-adjacent, opposite direction from CORE-10's originator advocacy); side-effect magnitudes not estimated; boundary conditions are the authors' own prescriptions, not tested.
- **Disconfirming Implication**: If goal setting's benefits hold without the named side effects under ordinary conditions (moderate goals, no reward link, user-set), the "over-prescribed" verdict is overstated; the article itself concedes the positive linear relation under boundary conditions (commitment, ability, no conflict).
- **H1**: partial — specific challenging goals boost performance (replicated); the row's warning concerns side effects.
- **H2**: yes — goal architecture is a vector (type, authorship, horizon, risk, culture), not a scalar difficulty dial.
- **Design Implication**: Goal failure modes → guardrails on the goal grammar (M4a): the tool's goal-setting layer must carry the warning-label logic — performance vs learning goals, user-set goals, time-horizon checks, risk articulation, intrinsic-motivation assessment, cooperation-aware goal units (Design Veto Catalogue, CF-1). Pairs with CORE-10's goal-grammar row.
- **Cosmotechnic Implication**: Goal narrowing as moral flattening — a goal scalar (output) that blinds the person to non-goal areas reproduces the Sears pattern (unethical shortcutting under goal pressure); S7 moral-hollowing guard (Tier-P).
- **Causal Status (draft)**: conceptual. **Evidence Type (draft)**: narrative review (advocacy-adjacent, critical stance).
- **Cosmo Rel.**: medium. **Notes for QC**: flag any draft phrase implying a magnitude; none intended.

## CORE-12 — Ferster & Skinner 1957 (Schedules of Reinforcement) — ABSTRACT-LEVEL (documented decision)
- **Key Finding / Thesis**: Charted at abstract/catalog level (archive lending only; no open full text found in audit pass; documented decision per handoff Step 3). The 741-page ONR-funded monograph (Harvard, 1949–1955) is the empirical foundation of schedule-controlled behavior: systematic mapping of how fixed/variable interval and ratio schedules and extinction control operant responding. The textbook summary (variable-ratio schedules are most extinction-resistant) is the standard reading of the book's findings but was NOT verified against text this pass.
- **Effect Size / Strength**: not_quantifiable — no numeric extraction at abstract level; book is an animal research monograph.
- **Limitations**: Full text unread (lending-only); animal subjects (pigeons/rats); 1957 methods; charting cannot verify specific schedule claims — the row's claims must stay at the catalog/documentary level (Tier-P).
- **Disconfirming Implication**: CORE-13's review (read this pass) shows schedule control is more complex than a single "VR most extinction-resistant" rule: fixed-interval scallop depends on absolute interval duration (timescale-invariance violations), matching is overdetermined, chain schedules can collapse. Any tool rule importing one schedule heuristic would overreach.
- **H1**: yes — contingency/schedule design shapes behavior (mechanism).
- **H2**: no.
- **Design Implication**: Contingency/schedule design (M4c) — import direction + bounded band only; never a point schedule rule; the tool's reward grammar must respect schedule dynamics (extinction, satiation, contrast) as failure modes.
- **Cosmotechnic Implication**: Slot-machine schedules risk dependency — variable-ratio reward architectures in a productivity tool are the dependency failure mode (CF-3); aligns with CORE-16's "self becomes captive of a certain kind of order" passage. Design veto territory.
- **Causal Status (draft)**: causal (lab-bounded, animal) as the source's nature — with explicit charting-base caveat (abstract-level; claims must not exceed the catalog/documentary base). **Evidence Type (draft)**: empirical (research monograph).
- **Cosmo Rel.**: medium. **Notes for QC**: Methodologist to adjudicate causal-status wording given abstract-level base.

## CORE-13 — Staddon & Cerutti 2003 (Operant conditioning review)
- **Key Finding / Thesis**: Operant behavior is behavior "controlled" by its consequences; the review documents lawful but complex schedule effects across interval timing (proportional timing, Weber's law, linear waiting) and choice (matching law and its extensions, concurrent chains, self-control). Discipline for design: consequence-engineering is not simple — matching is highly overdetermined (reveals little about underlying dynamics), molar response rate misses the fixed-interval scallop, multi-link chains can extinguish early links, and no integrated theory of conditioned reinforcement exists.
- **Effect Size / Strength**: not_quantifiable — narrative review; no pooled ES; the functional relations (matching, scalar property) are described qualitatively.
- **Limitations**: "This review is restricted to work with animals"; human operant results broadly similar but with weaker reinforcers and wider behavioral repertoires; several phenomena (chain-schedule collapse, concurrent fixed-interval) lack formal theory; author-manuscript version (NIHMS), review literature to ~2001.
- **Disconfirming Implication**: Timescale invariance is only approximately true (fixed-interval curvature depends on absolute interval length); matching maximization fails on simple RID schedules; chain responding can extinguish — schedule control has documented failure modes that naive contingency design would miss.
- **H1**: yes — schedules/contingencies control behavior.
- **H2**: no.
- **Design Implication**: Temper naive contingency design — the tool must not import simple reward/punish rules as calibrated scalars; consequences are powerful but non-obvious in their dynamics; use direction + bounded band; watch extinction/satiation dynamics (Motivation Crowding Watch Card, CF-3).
- **Cosmotechnic Implication**: Consequence engineering has hidden costs — a behaviorist reward grammar applied to the person is the manipulation paradigm (HUI-2024 cybernetics guard; consistent with batch-2 CORE-09 refusal: the loop must not treat the person as the plant). Consequences as mirror, not leash (Tier-P).
- **Causal Status (draft)**: correlational (narrative review) — the review synthesizes causal experiments but itself is a review (citation-is-not-evidence; batch-1 CORE-06 precedent). **Evidence Type (draft)**: narrative review (non-systematic; Annual Review chapter).
- **Cosmo Rel.**: low. **Notes for QC**: seeded "causal" likely overstates — Methodologist to confirm downgrade.

## CORE-14 — Clark & Chalmers 1998 (The Extended Mind)
- **Key Finding / Thesis**: Cognition is partly constituted by body, tools, and environment (active externalism). Parity principle: if a part of the world functions as a process which, were it done in the head, we would have no hesitation in recognizing as part of the cognitive process, then that part of the world is part of the cognitive process ("cognitive processes ain't (all) in the head"). Otto's notebook plays the role of memory — the extended mind; the extended self follows; interfering with someone's environment can have the same moral significance as interfering with their person.
- **Effect Size / Strength**: not_quantifiable — philosophical argument (thought experiments, parity intuitions).
- **Limitations**: Philosophical thesis, not empirical result; contested (internalist critiques; belief-criteria conditions — trust, constancy, endorsement — delimit the claim); the notebook-as-belief requires the information to be "a constant" reliably consulted; no experimental test offered.
- **Disconfirming Implication**: If the notebook fails the belief criteria (untrusted, rarely consulted, not endorsed), it is not part of belief — the authors set these conditions themselves; empirical findings that cognitive offloading does not yield the predicted effects would weaken the constitutive (not merely causal) claim.
- **H1**: partial — environment design shapes cognition.
- **H2**: yes — cognitive design is world-design (vector: ecology, technology, locality, reversibility).
- **Design Implication**: Warrant for treating environment design as cognitive design — the tool's capture architecture, external memory, and ambient structure ARE part of the user's cognitive system (Digital Capture Architecture); but the environment is the user's locality: the tool must remain a revision-capable prosthesis, not a replacement (S10; HUI-2024 prosthesis-vs-replacement gate).
- **Cosmotechnic Implication**: Cognitive design is also world-design — externalization is not neutral; designing the cognitive environment is designing the person's world (organology/proportion per HUI-2024); extended mind licenses locality, not colonization.
- **Causal Status (draft)**: conceptual. **Evidence Type (draft)**: philosophical.
- **Cosmo Rel.**: high. **Notes for QC**: Purist attention — the world-design claim must stay criterion-derived (Tier-P), not asserted as Hui's finding.

## CORE-16 — Csikszentmihalyi 1990 (Flow) — full book read (course-hosted mirror; rights caveat; archive lending stays canonical)
- **Key Finding / Thesis**: Optimal experience (flow) occurs when attention is ordered: information congruent with goals → psychic energy flows effortlessly (opposite of psychic entropy). Phenomenological conditions: clear goals, immediate feedback, deep but effortless involvement, sense of control, challenges matched to skills ("golden ratio" of challenge-skill balance), autotelic quality (the experience is an end in itself). Flow builds a more complex self; the conditions are suppliable by activities/environments; the state is not forceable. Flow activities can become addictive — "the self becomes captive of a certain kind of order, and is then unwilling to cope with the ambiguities of life."
- **Effect Size / Strength**: not_quantifiable — theoretical synthesis grounded in ESM/experience-sampling research; no pooled ES in the book.
- **Limitations**: Theoretical + descriptive (ESM surveys, interviews); individual differences (autotelic personality); the enjoyment/pleasure distinction is conceptual; the addiction passage is the phenomenon's own shadow side (dependency, escapism, narrowed attention); retrospective/self-report base.
- **Disconfirming Implication**: If challenge-skill balance is neither necessary nor sufficient (flow reported in low-challenge or feedback-free contexts), the conditions claim is incomplete; the addiction passage shows flow can capture rather than liberate — a tool that manufactures flow-like reward states reproduces the captive-self failure mode.
- **H1**: yes — flow is an achievable optimal-experience state.
- **H2**: partial — flow as attunement (vector), never extraction (scalar output).
- **Design Implication**: Conditions suppliable; state not forceable — the tool can supply flow's conditions (clear goals, immediate feedback, calibrated challenge) but must not command or extract the state; forcing flow = extraction (S1 violation); challenge calibration is person-side, never tool-side (A9-06 person-designated principle carries).
- **Cosmotechnic Implication**: Flow as attunement, not extraction — flow is the image of harmonious absorption; the tool cultivates the person's own ordering of attention; the addiction passage is the anti-veneer test (a tool manufacturing dependency on flow-like rewards fails the criterion).
- **Causal Status (draft)**: conceptual. **Evidence Type (draft)**: theoretical.
- **Cosmo Rel.**: medium. **Notes for QC**: Phenomenologist REQUIRED (archetypal phenomenological row); capture-order: felt experience first, calibration second.

## CORE-17 — Kazdin 1982 (Single-Case Research Designs) — ABSTRACT-LEVEL (documented decision)
- **Key Finding / Thesis**: Charted at abstract level (documented decision): the 1982 book (archive lending only) is the canonical methods text for SCEDs — multiple-baseline and related designs control history/maturation without a control person, licensing within-case causal inference. Corroborated at abstract level by Kazdin 2021 (JEAB 115(1):56-85, paywalled body): core features of SCEDs, how key/ancillary features evolved, special strengths, and challenges impeding integration.
- **Effect Size / Strength**: not_quantifiable — methods source.
- **Limitations**: Abstract-level charting (full texts gated); 1982 edition vs later editions differ (Google Books shows a later third edition); SCED inference is per-case (no population generalization); visual analysis + statistical power debates are live challenges (Kazdin 2021 abstract).
- **Disconfirming Implication**: SCED causal claims are contested (the "challenges" Kazdin names); without a no-reward baseline phase, no causal inference (batch-1/2 pilot rule); N-of-1 evidence cannot license population claims.
- **H1**: partial — SCED is the Tier-C pilot's inference spine.
- **H2**: no.
- **Design Implication**: Spine of the Tier-C pilot (SCED): phase logic = baseline → intervention → follow-up; multiple-baseline across behaviors; reporting per SCRIBE (CORE-18); claims bounded to the individual (Tier-P).
- **Cosmotechnic Implication**: Rigorous N-of-1 inference vs false generalization — single-case rigor honors the person's singularity; the pilot must never present per-case results as population-causal.
- **Causal Status (draft)**: conceptual (methods source; executed SCEDs license per-case causal claims). **Evidence Type (draft)**: methods.
- **Cosmo Rel.**: low. **Notes for QC**: Methodologist to adjudicate the per-case causal wording.

## CORE-18 — Tate et al. 2016 (SCRIBE 2016)
- **Key Finding / Thesis**: SCRIBE 2016 = a 26-item checklist for reporting single-case research, developed through 2 Delphi surveys + a 2-day expert consensus meeting (CONSORT-family process), published simultaneously in 9 journals; purpose: clear, complete, accurate reporting (title/abstract through results/discussion); explicitly NOT design/analysis guidance.
- **Effect Size / Strength**: not_quantifiable — reporting guideline.
- **Limitations**: Reporting standards do not guarantee design validity ("does not make recommendations about how to design, conduct or analyze"); consensus/Delphi-based (expert judgment, not empirical test); checklist burden on authors.
- **Disconfirming Implication**: A fully SCRIBE-compliant report of a poorly designed SCED is still poor evidence — reporting quality ≠ evidence quality (the reporting-guideline version of citation-is-not-evidence).
- **H1**: partial — the pilot's reporting follows SCRIBE.
- **H2**: no.
- **Design Implication**: Governs SCED reporting quality — the pilot's Preregistration Packet and final report must address the 26 items; transparency as the anti-self-deception mechanism.
- **Cosmotechnic Implication**: Transparency as anti-self-deception — reporting discipline makes the tool's own claims auditable (the person can see the record; reversibility S10).
- **Causal Status (draft)**: conceptual. **Evidence Type (draft)**: methods.
- **Cosmo Rel.**: low.

## CORE-19 — Klasnja et al. 2015 (Micro-randomized trials) — URL REPLACED 2026-08-13 (seeded PMC URL pointed at the wrong article)
- **Key Finding / Thesis**: MRT = experimental design for optimizing JITAIs: participants are microrandomized at decision points to intervention components; enables causal modeling of proximal effects of individual components and time-varying effect moderation (when and for whom components work); overcomes RCT limitations (whole-package evaluation) and factorial-design limitations for adaptive interventions.
- **Effect Size / Strength**: not_quantifiable — methods paper (HeartSteps used as illustration, not results).
- **Limitations**: Methods overview (no primary results); causal claims are proximal, not distal; requires sensing/delivery infrastructure; assumptions about no carryover/engagement effects must be checked; optimization of components ≠ optimization of the whole ecology.
- **Disconfirming Implication**: If component effects are not time-varying or carryover/engagement effects violate MRT assumptions, estimates are biased; MRT optimizes components — a separate confirming phase (CORE-20) is still required for the full system.
- **H1**: partial — the pilot's component testing uses MRT logic.
- **H2**: no.
- **Design Implication**: Best for high-frequency digital prompts/JITAI — the tool's component testing (Digital Capture Architecture) uses microrandomization for proximal-effect attribution; full-system claims require a confirming phase (CORE-20).
- **Cosmotechnic Implication**: Rigor for the digital channel — honest causal attribution of digital components serves the person; but component optimization must never optimize the person's attention away (S1/S10 guard; the tool stays a locality, not an optimizer of the user).
- **Causal Status (draft)**: conceptual (methods source; executed MRTs license causal claims for proximal effects). **Evidence Type (draft)**: methods.
- **Cosmo Rel.**: low. **Notes for QC**: seeded "causal" reflects the design's capability, not the source's own status — Methodologist to adjudicate.

## CORE-20 — Collins, Murphy & Strecher 2007 (MOST/SMART)
- **Key Finding / Thesis**: MOST = three-phase strategy for building/evaluating behavioral interventions: screening (efficiently identify active components), refining (fine-tune components/dosage), confirming (standard randomized confirmatory trial of the optimized package); SMART = design for building adaptive interventions (identify tailoring variables and decision rules empirically). Both use randomized experimentation for valid inference — an alternative to the slow RCT → post-hoc → revision cycle.
- **Effect Size / Strength**: not_quantifiable — methods overview.
- **Limitations**: Methods overview; authors note the methods were "relatively untried at this writing" (2007); component-by-component optimization can miss interactions not captured in screening; confirming phase still requires a full RCT; optimization is component-level, not ecology-level.
- **Disconfirming Implication**: If component effects interact non-additively beyond what factorial screening captures, the optimized package underperforms; MOST optimizes components — the tool must not mistake component optimization for ecosystem cultivation.
- **H1**: partial — the pilot's Intervention Component Library follows MOST.
- **H2**: no.
- **Design Implication**: Channel attribution once components defined — the pilot's component library + attribution logic follows MOST's screening → refining → confirming sequence (directly feeds the Tier-C pilot; handoff flags Methodologist attention on the optimization-trial logic).
- **Cosmotechnic Implication**: Avoid confounding the whole ecology — the tool must not optimize the person's total environment as if it were a component set; the ecology is the person's locality, not the optimizer's (S1; HUI-2024 locality/localism guard).
- **Causal Status (draft)**: conceptual (methods source; executed MOST confirmation licenses causal claims for the optimized package). **Evidence Type (draft)**: methods.
- **Cosmo Rel.**: low. **Notes for QC**: same adjudication as CORE-19.
