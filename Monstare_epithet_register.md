# EPITHET REGISTER
**Monstare Evidence Matrix — A Living Document**

---

## Purpose

This register preserves the voice of each charted author — not just their claims, but their tonal signature, their most concentrated phrases, and the felt sense of what they are pointing at. Downstream artifacts (Design Veto Catalogue, Cosmotechnic Audit Card, pilot rules) should be able to draw from this register so that a rule grounded in Heidegger *sounds* Heideggerian, not flattened into generic management-speak.

For each author, the register contains:
- **Epithet** — the 1-3 word signature that calls up the whole body
- **Quotations** — a range from subtle (quiet, precise, understated) to robust (prophetic, sweeping, declarative); the tonal register is exemplified, not described
- **Reverie** — an evocative vignette conveying the full spectrum of feelings and thoughts the concept evokes

---

## Example Entry — HEIDEGGER, *The Question Concerning Technology* (1949/1977)

**Epithet:** Enframing (Gestell)

**Quotations:**

> "The essence of technology is nothing technological."

> "Technology is therefore no mere means. Technology is a way of revealing."

> "Wherever ends are pursued and means are employed, wherever instrumentality reigns, there reigns causality."

> "Where everything that presences exhibits itself in the light of a cause-effect coherence, even God can lose all that is exalted."

> "Man stands so decisively in attendance on the challenging-forth of Enframing that he does not apprehend Enframing as a claim, that he fails to see himself as the one spoken to."

**Reverie:**

A hydroelectric plant is set into the current of the Rhine. The river is dammed up into the power works. What the river is now — a water power supplier — derives from out of the essence of the power station. The Rhine as uttered out of Hölderlin's hymn is gone. The revealing that rules in modern technology is a challenging — it puts to nature the unreasonable demand that it supply energy that can be extracted and stored. Unlocking, transforming, storing, distributing, switching about. But Enframing conceals not only bringing-forth; it conceals revealing itself. Where danger is, grows the saving power also.

## Example Entry — BORGMANN, *Technology and the Character of Contemporary Life* (1984)

**Epithet:** Device Paradigm

**Quotations:**

> "The device paradigm is the defining structure of modern technological culture."

> "A device is a means that makes the end available without engagement."

> "Focal things and practices center and order our lives in a profoundly different way."

> "The engagement with technology is the engagement with the world."

> "We are surrounded by commodified outputs stripped of the practices that would make them meaningful."

**Reverie:**

The fireplace demands wood, demands tending, demands presence. You split the log, arrange the kindling, strike the match. The fire catches. The family gathers — not because the fire is the occasion, but because the fire requires attention, and attention shared becomes communion. The central heat makes warmth available without engagement. The commodity arrives. The practice is gone. The center is gone. We are left with outputs without orbit, comfort without center, consumption without meaning. A focal practice is contestable — its meaning is not fixed but open to interpretation and dispute. The family meal is an argument about what matters. The device delivers a commodity and ends the conversation.

## Example Entry — HARAWAY, *A Cyborg Manifesto* (1985/1991)

**Epithet:** Cyborg

**Quotations:**

> "A cyborg is a cybernetic organism, a hybrid of machine and organism."

> "By the late twentieth century, our time, a mythic time, we are all chimeras."

> "The cyborg is a creature in a post-gender world; it has no truck with bisexuality, pre-oedipal symbiosis, unalienated labour, or other seductions to organic wholeness."

> "Cyborg imagery can suggest a way out of the maze of dualisms in which we have explained our bodies and our tools to ourselves."

> "Though both are bound in the spiral dance, I would rather be a cyborg than a goddess."

**Reverie:**

The boundary between human and animal is thoroughly breached. The last beachheads of uniqueness have been polluted. We are chimeras, theorized and fabricated hybrids of machine and organism. The cyborg has no origin story in the Western sense — no Garden of Eden, no phallic mother, no oedipal project. It skips the step of original unity. It is not made of mud and cannot dream of returning to dust. Cyborgs are not reverent; they do not re-member the cosmos. They are wary of holism but needy for connection. Pleasure in the confusion of boundaries. Responsibility in their construction. A world without gender, perhaps a world without genesis, but maybe also a world without end.
