## 3. Fault lines (must not be smoothed over)

1. **Simondon vs. Winner on the "nature" of technical objects:** Simondon treats technical objects as evolving relational beings with an *internal* genesis (concretization). Winner treats artifacts as *bearers of external* political priorities (inscription). These are not contradictory but they pull in different directions: Simondon's internal normativity says the tool has its own logic; Winner's inscription thesis says the tool carries its maker's politics. A tool that is highly "concrete" by Simondon's criterion could still be politically alienating by Winner's. *Resolution:* the two criteria are orthogonal — apply both.

2. **Stiegler vs. Heidegger on the "origin" of technics:** Stiegler (following Leroi-Gourhan) says technics is co-originary with the human — there is no pre-technical interior. Heidegger (in the 1949 lecture) says Greek *technē* (bringing-forth) is a *different* revealing from modern Gestell, but he preserves a privileged pre-modern relation to technics. Stiegler would read Heidegger's nostalgia for *poiesis* as the "originary forgetting" that Epimetheus names. *Resolution:* hold the tension. Stiegler is the more radical claim; Heidegger is the historical origin-point.

3. **Hui vs. Haraway on "cosmos" and boundaries:** Hui's cosmotechnics requires a cosmos-morality-technics unification — *some* cosmology, even if plural. Haraway's cyborg explicitly refuses cosmos ("they do not re-member the cosmos," "the cyborg would not recognize the Garden of Eden"). Hui needs a cosmos to unify; Haraway's cyborg *is* the breakdown of cosmos. *Resolution:* Haraway is a CRITIQUE row (standpoint reorientation); Hui is a TELOS row (positive criterion). They operate at different levels — but the tension is live and must not be smoothed.

4. **Latour vs. Simondon on the "agency" of objects:** Latour's missing-masses thesis says non-humans are actors in the social bond (they delegate, prescribe, shape conduct). Simondon's concretization thesis says technical objects have an internal genesis with a margin of indeterminacy that requires human interpretation. Latour is *social*; Simondon is *ontological*. They ask different questions. *Resolution:* Latour tells you what the tool does *in the social bond*; Simondon tells you what the tool *is in its genesis*. Both matter for design.

5. **A1-12 Latour synopsis-level caveat:** The charting is based on the landing-page synopsis, not the full chapter. The "groom is on strike" anecdote and the missing-masses thesis are clear, but the developed argument (the semiotic analysis of delegation, the "program" vs. "anti-program" distinction) is NOT in front of me. This is the sub-batch's largest verification debt.

6. **A1-04 partial-text caveat:** The edited volume's framing is clear from the TOC, but no chapter author's argument is in front of me. Charting is at the volume's thesis-statement level — upgradeable if/when the user supplies the full introduction.

## 4. Tier-C pilot implications (binding)

- **Openness over automatism (A1-05 Simondon):** The tool's degree of technicity is measured by its margin of indeterminacy and openness to outside information, NOT by its automation level. A "smart" tool that closes the user out of its operation is *less* technically advanced than a "dumb" tool the person can interpret and adapt. Design implication: the tool must preserve and display its own operation so the person can coordinate it — conductor-orchestra, not master-slave.

- **Pharmacological framing of AI/memory (A1-07 Stiegler):** External memory and AI assistance are *pharmakon* from the start — remedy AND poison simultaneously, not neutral tools that become addictive through misuse. The design question is dosage: what exteriorization constitutes the human *as* this human, and what dissolves it? The artifact must preserve the freedom to return from its own pharmacological effect (S10 reversibility).

- **Cosmotechnic pluralism (A1-02 Hui):** The tool's design must answer to a cosmotechnic criterion internal to its milieu — not a universal "best practice" exported from one technical culture. Refuse one productivity ontology. This is a design veto on any feature that silently imposes a single cosmos-ethic-technics unity.

- **Artifacts have politics (A1-11 Winner):** The Design Veto Catalogue must ask of every artifact: what politics does it inscribe? Does it require centralized, authoritarian, or minority-entrenching organization to function? If so, veto. This is now a named criterion in the CF-10 slot.

- **Enframing guard (A1-15 Heidegger):** Never let the tool reduce the person to standing-reserve (human resources, attention-mining, engagement-optimization). The artifact must preserve the freedom to step back from enframing (S10). Art/poiesis as counter-mode is a named design resource.
