## 3. Key cross-area findings

**Phenomenology-before-calibration is now operationalized (Area 5):**
Varela (neurophenomenology) + Petitmengin (second-person interview) + Hurlburt (DES) + Bass-Krueger (DES vs MPI comparison) together provide a complete methodological toolkit for capturing first-person experience. The Phenomenological Registration Sheet can now draw on: random beeping (DES), episode-evoking prompts (MPI), and the mutual-constraints framework (neurophenomenology).

**Ritual is empirically grounded (Area 8):**
Norton & Gino (control restoration through ritual) + Tian et al. (self-discipline through ritual) provide causal evidence that rituals work. Van Gennep (separation-liminality-incorporation) + Turner (liminality and communitas) provide the structural schema. Bell (ritual as strategic) provides the caution: ritual is not innocent.

**Agency is designable but vague (Area 3):**
Bennett et al. show that "agency" and "autonomy" are umbrella constructs in HCI — used interchangeably and rarely defined. Baumer & Silberman provide the radical option: sometimes the right move is not to design. The Agency & Autonomy Checklist must require explicit definitions.

**Personal Informatics is extractive by default (Area 2):**
Lupton (datafication of selfhood) + Selke (surveillance implications) + Sharon (empowerment vs discipline) together show that self-tracking is not neutral. The Digital Capture Architecture must be designed against extraction.

**Nature restores attention (Area 4):**
Kaplan & Kaplan (restorative environments) + Berman et al. (cognitive benefits of nature) provide the evidence base for nature-restoration features. Gibson (affordances) provides the theoretical grounding.
