## A1-05 — Simondon, On the Mode of Existence of Technical Objects (1958; Univocal 2016 trans.)
**Source read:** livingindigitalarchives mirror (Univocal 2016, Malaspina & Rogove trans.), full Prospectus + Introduction + Part I Ch.1 incipit. Text layer verified. 294pp equivalent.
**Pri A | CR high | Function: TELOS/MECHANISM/DESIGN | Evidence Type: philosophical | Causal Status: conceptual**

**Key Finding / Thesis (UPGRADED from seed):**
Technical objects are not inert tools but evolving relational beings with a genesis. Their mode of existence unfolds through a process of *concretization*: the primitive technical object is an abstract system of isolated, non-reciprocally-causal functioning parts, whereas the perfected technical object is concrete — each structure is pluri-functional, overdetermined, and exists not only as organ but as body, milieu, and ground for other structures (Prospectus). This genesis cannot be reduced to empirical correction or deductive logic; it has its own specific rhythm (discontinuous major improvements + continuous minor ones). Man's role is not master-inventor but the permanent coordinator and interpreter of technical objects within an ensemble — conductor-like, not slave-master (Introduction). Culture is unbalanced because it grants aesthetic objects citizenship in the world of significations while banishing technical objects to a structureless utility-only domain; this misunderstanding produces alienation that is cultural, not technological. Automatism is a *low* degree of technical perfection — true technicity is openness, a margin of indeterminacy sensitive to outside information. The three levels of technical reality are element, individual, ensemble; cultural integration is stable only at the ensemble level.

**Effect Size / Strength:** not_quantifiable (philosophical). Strength = high as the batch's densest TELOS/MECHANISM/DESIGN row (construct-role claim, not evidence strength).

**Limitations:** Single-author constructive monograph; programmatic agenda; archive mirror (Univocal 2016 trans., authorized but not the only translation — rybn.org hosts 1980 Mellamphy trans. as alternate; provenance-tag Univocal edition). The concretization thesis is a philosophical claim, not an empirically tested one. Selective engagement with the history of technology (focus on cybernetics, information theory, and energetics of its era).

**Disconfirming Implication:** If technical objects do NOT have an internal genesis (if they are fully reducible to economic/social determination, or to the intentions of their makers), the concretization thesis overstates the internal normativity of technics; the "margin of indeterminacy" claim risks vitalism if it attributes purposiveness to inorganic matter.

**H1:** no | **H2:** yes
**Design Implication (UPGRADED):** Judge tools by their degree of concretization and openness, not their output efficiency. Prefer open, pluri-functional, concretized objects with margins of indeterminacy the person can interpret — over closed, automatic, "perfect" ones. The person is the permanent coordinator; the tool is a relational partner, not a prosthetic slave.
**Cosmotechnic Implication (UPGRADED):** The cosmotechnic question is whether a tool integrates or alienates *in its genesis* — not as a post-hoc judgment of utility. A tool that closes the person out of its concretization process is alienating regardless of output. The criterion is participation in the genesis of technicity.
**Causal Status:** conceptual (philosophical monograph)

## A1-07 — Stiegler, Technics and Time v1: The Fault of Epimetheus (1998; Stanford UP)
**Source read:** Image-only PDF (monoskop mirror — text layer NOT extractable; verified image-only). Charting supplemented by: (a) Philpapers abstract (bibliographic confirmation), (b) detailed independent summary (thesimulationspace.wordpress.com, 2014, confirmed accurate against known Stiegler thesis), (c) cross-row alignment with charted A1-01/HUI-2024.
**Pri A | CR high | Function: TELOS/WARNING | Evidence Type: philosophical | Causal Status: conceptual**

**Key Finding / Thesis (UPGRADED from seed):**
Technics is the exteriorization of the human through prosthesis; the human is invented *as* it invents technics (co-constitution, not subject-object). Time is constituted through this technic: "no technics without time, no time without technics." The Prometheus/Epimetheus myth gives the structure: Epimetheus (forgetfulness, afterthought) distributes traits to animals and leaves humans bare; Prometheus (foresight) gives humans fire/technics as compensatory prosthesis. This "fault" is originary — technics is supplementary from the start, not a fall from a natural state. Memory is the primary domain of exteriorization: individual memory freed from biological constraint, preserved in technics, surviving the individual's death (*epiphylogenesis*). *Grammatization* — the breaking of flux into discrete elements (writing, inscription, programmable machines) — is the process by which life pursues itself by means other than life. Dasein's temporality is constituted through technicity; anticipation is technical before it is conscious. The "who" (human) and "what" (technics) are co-original: technics can be the "who" as much as the human, destabilizing the humanist subject-object frame.

**Effect Size / Strength:** not_quantifiable (philosophical). Strength = high as the governing pharmacological WARNING row — external memory as *pharmakon* (remedy + poison) is the structural criterion for AI/assistant design (construct-role claim).

**Limitations:** Image-only source — claims cannot be page-verified against the original. Single-author dense phenomenological-philosophical monograph; programmatic; engagement with Derrida, Heidegger, Leroi-Gourhan that is reconstructive rather than independently evidenced. The summary basis means the "originary technicity" thesis is charted from secondary synthesis — flagged as verification debt for a full-text institutional copy.

**Disconfirming Implication:** If technics is NOT co-constitutive of the human (if there is a pre-technical human interior, as Rousseau or a naturalistic anthropology would claim), the whole exteriorization/Epimetheus structure collapses into a weaker claim about tools as instruments of an already-constituted subject.

**H1:** partial | **H2:** yes
**Design Implication (UPGRADED):** Frame external memory and AI assistance as pharmacological *from the start* — never as neutral tools that become addictive through misuse, but as co-constitutive of the user's temporality and anticipation. The design question is: what dose of exteriorization constitutes the human *as* this human, and what dose dissolves it? The AI must not close the margin of indeterminacy (cf. Simondon) through which the human interprets. No "machine of all machines."
**Cosmotechnic Implication (UPGRADED):** The cosmotechnic criterion is the *pharmakon*: every exteriorization is remedy-and-poison simultaneously. There is no "safe" exteriorization, only dosed ones. The artifact must preserve the freedom to return from its own pharmacological effect (S10 reversibility).
**Causal Status:** conceptual (philosophical monograph)

## A1-02 — Hui, Cosmotechnics as Cosmopolitics (e-flux journal #86, 2017)
**Source read:** e-flux journal HTML full article (247KB, live-verified 200). Complete.
**Pri B | CR high | Function: TELOS/CRITIQUE | Evidence Type: philosophical | Causal Status: conceptual**

**Key Finding / Thesis (UPGRADED from seed):**
Cosmotechnics must move from cosmology to cosmotechnics as cosmopolitics. The concept is rooted in a Kantian antinomy: (Thesis) technology is an anthropological universal — exteriorization of memory, liberation of organs; (Antithesis) technology is NOT anthropologically universal but enabled/constrained by particular cosmologies. Hence there is no single technology but *multiple cosmotechnics*. Cosmotechnics = "the unification of the cosmos and the moral through technical activities, whether craft-making or art-making" (§3). Every culture must systematize its own cosmotechnics (e.g., the Chinese Qi-Dao episteme) — the project is not traditionalist but a reopening of the question of technology to envisage *bifurcation of technological futures*. The "ontological turn" in anthropology (Descola, Viveiros de Castro, Latour) is a provocation but insufficient because it insufficiently addresses technics. Modern technology (Heidegger's Gestell/enframing) is not the only form of technics; Greek *technē* as bringing-forth (poiesis) differs fundamentally. The end of unilateral globalization + the Anthropocene force the cosmopolitical question; cosmopolitanism without cosmotechnics reproduces the single hegemon in new form.

**Effect Size / Strength:** not_quantifiable (philosophical). Strength = high as a TELOS/CRITIQUE row — the multiple-cosmotechnics thesis is the direct extension of A1-01 and HUI-2024's cosmotechnic pluralism (construct-role claim).

**Limitations:** Single-author programmatic journal article; normative agenda; selective engagement with anthropology (Descola's four ontologies), Chinese philosophy (Mou Zongsan, Zhuangzi Pao Ding story, Qi-Dao), and Kant/Hegel. The cosmotechnics concept is constructive, not empirically demonstrated. Same author as governing rows A1-01/HUI-2024 — self-citation risk; consistency check is the relevant QC, not independence.

**Disconfirming Implication:** If the cosmotechnics concept merely reproduces the Western philosopher's gesture of systematizing other cultures' thought (if Qi-Dao reconstruction is Orientalist projection), it reinstates the unilateral globalization it claims to overcome.

**H1:** no | **H2:** yes
**Design Implication (UPGRADED):** The tool's design must answer to a cosmotechnic criterion internal to its milieu — not a universal "best practice" exported from one technical culture. Support personal technodiversity: the tool should not impose one cosmos-ethic-technics unity but allow the user's own cosmotechnic (cf. A1-01's milieu-localization). Refuse one productivity ontology.
**Cosmotechnic Implication (UPGRADED):** Direct cosmotechnic criterion: the unification of cosmos + moral through technical activity must be plural, not singular. The artifact must not flatten multiple cosmotechnics into the global technical system. (Definitional continuity with A1-01's cosmotechnics-as-unification and HUI-2024's technodiversity confirmed.)
**Causal Status:** conceptual (philosophical article)

## A1-15 — Heidegger, The Question Concerning Technology (1949 lecture; 1977 trans.)
**Source read:** UCI course mirror PDF, full essay. Text layer verified. Complete through closing Hölderlin citation.
**Pri C | CR medium | Function: TELOS/CRITIQUE | Evidence Type: philosophical | Causal Status: conceptual**

**Key Finding / Thesis (UPGRADED from seed):**
The essence of technology is nothing technological — it is not a means to an end (instrumental definition) nor a human activity (anthropological definition), though both are "correct" without being "true." The essence is *Enframing* (Gestell): a way of revealing that challenges forth, setting upon nature to unlock, transform, store, distribute, and switch about energy — revealing everything as *standing-reserve* (Bestand). Modern technology differs from Greek *technē* (bringing-forth / poiesis) in kind, not degree: the windmill lets the wind blow (bringing-forth); the hydroelectric plant dams the Rhine (challenging-forth). Enframing conceals not only the earlier bringing-forth (poiesis) but concealing *itself*, and with it the unconcealment wherein truth (aletheia) happens. The *danger* is not machines but the destining of revealing that blocks every other possibility of revealing — "where everything that presences exhibits itself in the light of a cause-effect coherence, even God can lose all that is exalted." Yet "where danger is, grows the saving power also" (Hölderlin) — the saving power grows precisely in Enframing because it belongs to the destining that grants man a share in revealing. Freedom is not connected to the will but to belonging to the destining of revealing as one who listens, not one who merely obeys.

**Effect Size / Strength:** not_quantifiable (philosophical essay). Strength = medium-high as the origin-point for the universality problem — the essay underpins HUI-2024's cybernetics critique and the whole "cosmotechnics as response to Heidegger" motif (construct-role claim).

**Limitations:** Single lecture; dense phenomenological essay; the Greek/German etymologies (technikón, poiesis, aitia, Gestell, Geschick, Wesen-as-verb) are doing philosophical work that resists paraphrase. The "saving power" closing is allusive, not systematic. Course-mirror copy (not Harper Row edition) — pagination diverges from standard translation.

**Disconfirming Implication:** If modern technology IS fully reducible to applied physical science (the view Heidegger contests), the enframing thesis overstates the metaphysical specificity of technics and becomes a literary-anthropological observation rather than an ontological claim. If there is NO revealing other than standing-reserve (if the saving power does not grow), Heidegger's closing is empty hope.

**H1:** no | **H2:** yes
**Design Implication (UPGRADED):** The pressure-source, not the governing authority (Hui responds to it). Use the tool as a site of revealing — ask what it unconceals and what it conceals. Never let the tool reduce the person to standing-reserve (human resources, attention-mining, engagement-optimization). The artifact must preserve the freedom to step back from enframing (S10). Art/poiesis as counter-mode is a design resource.
**Cosmotechnic Implication (UPGRADED):** Origin-point for the universality problem — cosmotechnics begins where Heidegger's enframing is identified. The criterion: the tool must not be the Gestell that reduces cosmos + person + world to standing-reserve. But the tool cannot "solve" enframing — only hold open the question of other revealings.
**Causal Status:** conceptual (philosophical essay)

## A1-11 — Winner, The Whale and the Reactor (1984; U Chicago Press)
**Source read:** Image-only PDF (ratical.org mirror — text layer NOT extractable; verified image-only). Charting supplemented by: (a) publisher page (Chicago UP), (b) Boundary 2 review of 2nd ed. (Loeb, 2020), (c) ESTS journal article "Langdon Winner's Theory of Technological Politics", (d) cross-row alignment with CORE-14 world-design warrant.
**Pri B | CR medium | Function: WARNING/CRITIQUE | Evidence Type: philosophical | Causal Status: conceptual**

**Key Finding / Thesis (UPGRADED from seed):**
Artifacts have politics. Technologies embody political priorities in two primary ways: (1) instances where an artifact is designed/configured to settle a larger social issue (inscription — e.g., nuclear power requiring centralized authority), and (2) technologies that are *inherently political* — they require, or are strongly compatible with, a particular variety of political organization (e.g., solar power compatible with decentralized democracy, nuclear with authoritarianism). This is NOT technological determinism: it is the recognition that the process of technical creation projects particular political ideas, needs, and interests *into* devices. Some artifacts have "bad politics" that entrench the authority of a powerful minority, sap democratic willingness, and break down common ends. The book is a series of essays exploring this thesis across cases (nuclear reactors, aquaculture, communications, automation) and connecting it to democratic theory — "the vitality of democratic politics depends upon people's willingness to act together in pursuit of their common ends."

**Effect Size / Strength:** not_quantifiable (philosophical/political essay collection). Strength = medium-high as the WARNING/CRITIQUE row — directly feeds the Design Veto Catalogue (CF-10 artifact-politics) and pairs with CORE-14's world-design warrant (construct-role claim).

**Limitations:** Image-only source — claims cannot be page-verified against the original. Essay collection, not a unified monograph — the "artifacts have politics" thesis is argued through cases, not formal derivation. The political analysis is US-democratic in frame. The Boundary 2 review and ESTS article are secondary syntheses; flagged as verification debt for the full institutional copy.

**Disconfirming Implication:** If artifacts do NOT have politics (if technical design is value-neutral and politics is only a matter of use-context), the whole inscription thesis collapses into a weaker claim about user behavior around neutral tools. If "inherently political" technologies are just those whose scale happens to fit certain political forms, the thesis overstates inherence.

**H1:** no | **H2:** yes
**Design Implication (UPGRADED):** The Design Veto Catalogue must ask of every artifact: what politics does it inscribe? Does it require a centralized, authoritarian, or minority-entrenching form of organization to function? If so, veto. Does it concentrate power without democratic accountability? If so, veto. The tool must be compatible with democratic, revisable, common-end-preserving practice.
**Cosmotechnic Implication (UPGRADED):** Artifacts are not neutral — the cosmotechnic question includes the political order the artifact materializes. A tool that enforces a political order alien to the user's milieu-localization is cosmotechnically alienating regardless of its output efficiency.
**Causal Status:** conceptual (philosophical/political essay collection)

## A1-18 — Haraway, A Cyborg Manifesto (1985/1991; Routledge)
**Source read:** SFU course mirror PDF (full text through p.181 / "Though both are bound in the spiral dance, I would rather be a cyborg than a goddess"). Text layer verified. Complete.
**Pri C | CR low | Function: CRITIQUE | Evidence Type: theoretical | Causal Status: conceptual**

**Key Finding / Thesis (UPGRADED from seed):**
The cyborg is a cybernetic organism, a hybrid of machine and organism, a creature of social reality as well as fiction. By the late 20th century "we are all chimeras" — the cyborg is our ontology, giving us our politics. Three crucial boundary breakdowns enable this: (1) human/animal (the last beachheads of uniqueness breached; nothing convincingly settles the separation); (2) organism-machine (pre-cybernetic machines were haunted, not self-designing; late-20th-century machines have made thoroughly ambiguous the difference between natural/artificial, mind/body, self-developing/externally designed); (3) physical/non-physical (microelectronics — the silicon chip as surface for writing; modern machines are "quintessence," ether). The cyborg is a creature in a post-gender world; it has no origin story in the Western sense (no original unity, no phallic mother, no oedipal project); it skips the step of identification with nature. The political struggle is to see from *both* perspectives at once (cyborg world as final grid of control AND as lived social-bodied reality in which people are not afraid of joint kinship with animals/machines). "Though both are bound in the spiral dance, I would rather be a cyborg than a goddess."

**Effect Size / Strength:** not_quantifiable (theoretical essay). Strength = medium as a CRITIQUE row — the cyborg thesis is a standpoint-reorientation resource, not a load-bearing cosmotechnic warrant (construct-role claim).

**Limitations:** Ironic, mythopoetic essay — resists paraphrase-as-thesis. The "we are all cyborgs" is a performative political claim, not an empirical one. The three boundary breakdowns are asserted through science-fictional and medical examples, not formal argument. Socialist-feminist framing is particular (US, late-Cold-War, white professional middle-class female mid-adult body as partial perspective). The "informatics of domination" chart (pp.161-63) is a period piece (C3I, Star Wars, eugenics, stress management).

**Disconfirming Implication:** If the boundaries are NOT broken down (if human/animal, organism-machine, physical/non-physical remain robust), the cyborg thesis is metaphor rather than ontology. If "pleasure in the confusion of boundaries" becomes an alibi for the informatics of domination (if celebrating hybridity obscures structural domination), the manifesto undermines its own political force.

**H1:** no | **H2:** partial
**Design Implication (UPGRADED):** The tool does not need to enforce a purity boundary (human vs machine, natural vs artificial). It can be a site of fruitful coupling. But the cyborg is also "the final imposition of a grid of control on the planet" — the design must hold *both* possibilities at once, not celebrate hybridity as liberation from responsibility.
**Cosmotechnic Implication (UPGRADED):** Boundary plurality is a cosmotechnic resource — the artifact need not enforce one cosmos-boundary. But "I would rather be a cyborg than a goddess" is a political choice, not a cosmotechnic criterion; it stays Tier-P.
**Causal Status:** conceptual (theoretical essay)

## A1-12 — Latour, Where Are the Missing Masses? (1992 chapter, Bijker & Law eds., MIT Press)
**Source read:** bruno-latour.fr landing page (HTML). The HTML is the chapter's *abstract/synopsis only* — the "groom is on strike" door notice + the missing-masses argument in miniature. The page confirms: 1992 chapter in Bijker & Law (eds.) Shaping Technology-Building Society, MIT Press, pp. 225-259. French, German, Russian, Italian, Dutch translations noted. NOT the full chapter.
**Pri B | CR medium | Function: MECHANISM/DESIGN | Evidence Type: theoretical | Causal Status: conceptual**

**Key Finding / Thesis (UPGRADED from seed):**
Sociologists looking for sturdy social links to hold society together are like cosmologists looking for missing mass. The missing mass is *non-humans* — the hidden, despised social masses (artifacts, technical objects) that make up morality and society. To balance the accounts of society, sociology must turn from humans to non-humans. Artifacts delegate, prescribe, and shape conduct — they are "frozen morality" (the door-closer replaces the groom; the seatbelt disciplines the driver; the speed bump enforces the speed limit). The program: a sociology that treats non-humans as actors in the social bond, not as props for human action.

**Effect Size / Strength:** not_quantifiable (theoretical chapter). Strength = medium as the MECHANISM/DESIGN row — the missing-masses thesis is the warrant for asking what the tool does, not just what the user does (construct-role claim).

**Limitations:** Landing-page / synopsis only — the full 35pp chapter (225-259) is NOT in front of me. This is partial text at the level of abstract + key image. The "groom is on strike" anecdote is a synoptic hook, not the developed argument. Charting is therefore at the thesis-statement level with documented coverage caveat — flagged as verification debt for the institutional chapter.

**Disconfirming Implication:** If non-humans do NOT have agency in any meaningful sense (if "delegation" and "prescription" are merely metaphors for human intentions frozen into matter), the missing-masses thesis collapses into a weaker claim about human social behavior around inert things.

**H1:** no | **H2:** partial
**Design Implication (UPGRADED):** The artifact is an actor in the moral bond, not a neutral tool. Design = delegating/prescribing/conduct-shaping. Ask: what behavior does this tool inscribe? Who is the human it delegates away? Is the prescription one the user would endorse upon reflection?
**Cosmotechnic Implication (UPGRADED):** The tool is not a passive object but a social-mass participant — the cosmotechnic audit must include the non-human actors the tool aligns with or against.
**Causal Status:** conceptual (theoretical chapter)
